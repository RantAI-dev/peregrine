## 0. The goal and what it takes

**Goal: the fastest CPU engine for embedding models and for decision models, against every
engine, at equal accuracy.** Not only llama.cpp: on small encoders the engines to beat are
OpenVINO and ONNX Runtime, which are about twice as fast as llama.cpp on our own baseline
(all-MiniLM-L6, bluefin, 4 threads: OpenVINO 655 texts/s at 16 tokens, 96.6 at 128, 20.5 at 512).

What the research says, in four sentences:

1. Their lead on **short texts** is batching and low fixed overhead; their lead on **long texts**
   (1.4-1.6×) is a register-blocked FMA matrix kernel with weights packed at load, and (OpenVINO
   only) attention computed in cache-sized strips. OpenVINO runs at roughly half of the 4-core FMA
   peak, so a perfect kernel alone buys parity plus perhaps 1.4×, not a multiple.
2. A **lead** therefore has to come from what a rectangular graph runtime cannot do: never
   computing padding (their batch is a rectangle padded to the longest text), attention per text
   with no mask, compact weights (F16, Q8_0) at full accuracy, one weight pass shared by mixed
   work, and exact reuse of shared prompt prefixes.
3. **Peregrine today** spends its time where the plan below aims: 83-86% of a causal embedder's
   pass is the Q8_0 matrix kernel; an encoder's pass is the matrix kernel plus a scalar attention
   loop (35% of the pass at 128 tokens, 60% at 512) plus a serial re-quantisation that is mostly
   redundant; a decision on a Qwen 3.5 model recomputes the shared state for every question.
4. For **decision models** llama.cpp is the only other engine; it shares the state between the
   questions of a request by forking the sequence (at most three children per prefix evaluation).
   Peregrine recomputes it. Forking the hybrid's state for every question at once is worth 2-10×
   by the shared fraction (a 300-token state with four 30-token questions: 1320 tokens of work
   become 420).

## 1. How "state of the art" is proved

A claim is made only for a row that has been measured on the harness, on the same machine, model
and thread count, against every engine in the table at its best documented setting.

- **Engines**: OpenVINO (fp32 and int8), ONNX Runtime through fastembed (fp32 and int8),
  text-embeddings-inference with its ONNX backend, llama.cpp v0.9.0 (F16 and Q8_0), PyTorch
  sentence-transformers as the accuracy reference; for decision models llama.cpp `/v1/systemone`.
- **Scenarios** (MLPerf-style): *single stream* (one short text at a time: p50 and p90 latency),
  *server* (Poisson arrivals from several clients: p99 latency at a throughput), *offline*
  (large batches: texts/s and tokens/s). Token lengths 16, 128, 512 and a **mixed-length** set
  (the missing case in the first baseline: every batch there had texts of one length, which hides
  the cost of padding). For decision models: 1, 3 and 10 questions on 64- to 4096-token states,
  cold and with a warm prefix.
- **Equal accuracy**: every row carries the cosine to the fp32 reference; a faster row with lower
  accuracy than ours is not a loss, and ours is never claimed against a row it is less accurate
  than. Retrieval quality is checked on NanoBEIR and four small BEIR sets (SciFact, NFCorpus,
  ArguAna, SCIDOCS), STS-B, and one rerank set; decision models by agreement with llama.cpp.
- **Machines**: bluefin (AVX2, 4 cores), the desktop (AVX-512 VNNI, 12 cores; note it runs 512-bit
  operations on a 256-bit datapath, so it understates wide-vector gains), the ARM host.
- The target per row: **≥ 1.0× the best competitor at equal or better accuracy**, and the
  published table shows every row, wins and losses.

## 2. Decisions

1. **A float kernel whose results do not depend on the batch.** The new F32/F16/BF16 kernel
   computes every output element as one sequential FMA chain over the input index (the weights
   are laid out so that each vector lane is a different output). Its value is then the same
   whatever tile, tail, block, thread split or vector width it was computed in: batch invariance
   by construction, and AVX2, AVX-512, NEON and a scalar `mul_add` reference agree bit for bit.
   The price: FMA and the new order change every float golden once.
2. **What "lossless" means for an encoder file.** An encoder's Q8_0 file is run as int8 weights
   times f32 activations: the exact reading of the file. Quantising the activations as well (what
   llama.cpp does, and what Peregrine's causal families do today) is the integer path; for
   encoders it becomes an option, on by default only where it has been shown to cost nothing
   measurable (to be decided per tier from S4.9k's report; on AVX2 it buys about 1.15× at best).
   The causal families keep their current arithmetic and goldens.
3. **No padding, ever.** Rows of different texts share the matrix kernel as they are; attention
   is per text. No length sorting, no bucketing.
4. **Batching without a timer.** Whatever is queued when the engine is free runs together; batch
   invariance makes the result independent of who shared the pass.
5. **Lossy accelerations are opt-in and reported** (ADR 0003): integer activations on encoders,
   output-vector quantisation, Matryoshka truncation beyond what the model card documents. Token
   pruning, early exit and static-embedding distillation are out of scope: they change the model.

## 5. Risks

- **The fp32 ceiling is close**: on fixed-length batches the best outcome against OpenVINO from
  kernels alone is parity to 1.4×. The lead must show on mixed lengths, long texts, compact
  weights and prefix reuse, and the proof run must include those rows.
- **Single-text latency against an int8 engine** on a small-cache machine may not be winnable
  with fp32 weights; the F16 or Q8_0 file with float activations is the likelier route.
- **Thread ownership per layer** multiplies weight traffic by the thread count: right for a
  batch, wrong for one short text; it must be chosen by the work, and it interacts with mixed
  chat and embedding passes.
- **Pack memory**: packed panels duplicate the mapped weights unless they replace them.
- **The recurrent fork** must carry everything a row's arithmetic reads (state, convolution
  history, rollback snapshot), or forked answers differ.
- The competitors were read at their main branches; the benchmarked wheels may differ. The proof
  run pins versions.

## 6. S4.9j as built (part 1: forking the state)

**What a snapshot carries.** `kv.rs`'s `KvSnapshot` packs the attention K/V rows of the first `len`
positions (f16, f32 or int8, in the stored layout) and — new — a `RecSnapshot`: each linear-attention
layer's Gated DeltaNet state and its convolution ring of the last `d_conv − 1` raw query/key/value
rows, in f32. That is everything a row at a position `≥ len` reads from the cache: the state after
row `len−1`, the ring, and the stored keys and values. The rollback checkpoint and the
within-forward speculation snapshot are *not* copied: they describe writes that will not happen in
the fork, and a fresh cache's rollback point is its own start.

A hybrid snapshot is restorable only at its own length. A position index cannot rewind the recurrent
state — it is a function of the whole prefix, not of a length — so `restore` refuses any other cut
(a dense snapshot still restores any length up to its own). `PrefixStore::best_where` takes a
predicate so the store can return the longest beginning the cache can actually use, instead of a
longer entry that would shadow a shorter, restorable one; `insert` keeps a hybrid entry when a longer
entry extends it (the longer snapshot cannot serve the shorter beginning).

**Where the fork happens.** `KvCache::fork_from(parent, len)` is a memcpy of those rows and buffers
between two caches of the same geometry and precision; no arithmetic runs. `Engine::admit_labels_group`
computes the group's longest common prompt beginning `p` (capped at `min(len_i) − 1`, so every
question keeps a row of its own for the read-out), admits the first question's slot as the leader and
the others as waiting followers, and caps the leader's prefix-store lookup at `p`. The leader's
`prefill_len` is `p` until it gets there, so the ordinary mixed scheduler forwards `0..p` once —
beside other users' decode rows, in the usual chunks — and `fork_groups` then copies its state into
each follower (setting their `fed`/`pos` to `p`) and lets every tail continue in shared passes. The
leader is question 0, not a parent slot of its own: a parent would need one more full cache (and its
recurrent state) for `p` positions, and the group already has a slot that wants exactly those rows.
When the leader already holds `p` at admission — the prefix store had the shared beginning — the
fork is done at admission. A group wider than the slots is admitted in several groups; each
re-forwards the beginning unless the store has it, which is why the fork also offers the shared
beginning to the store (the snapshot's buffers are reserved at admission, so the fork allocates
nothing).

**Why a fork is bit-identical.** The copy is exact: the recurrent state and conv ring are f32 held in
the cache, and K/V bytes are already rounded to the cache's precision on write, so a forked child's
stored state at `p` is bit-for-bit what the parent has. From there the engine's chunk invariance
does the rest: a row's arithmetic depends only on its position and the stored state, never on the
pass it landed in. Attention partitions a row's visible positions by that row's own context
(`attn::row_chunks`, pool- and batch-independent) and `attn::run_rows` recomputes the same partials
and merges them in the same order when a task carries several rows; the Gated DeltaNet steps are
per-row functions of the row and its cache's state (the conv walks rows of a cache in order; the
delta rule is per head a function of the state and the row). So a fork at `p` behaves exactly like a
prefill chunk boundary at `p`, which `prefill_chunk.rs` keeps bit-identical. The matrix in
`crates/peregrine-models/tests/labels.rs` pins it: groups of 2/3/10 with tails of different lengths,
`p` at 1, at a chunk boundary, mid-chunk and one row before the end, f16 and f32 caches, chunks
1/16/64, 1/2/4 threads, synthetic `llama` and `qwen35`, and beside chat and embedding slots. No
engine quantity had to change for it: the candidates from the plan (K/V rounded on write only, the
recurrent state checkpointed per chunk, the conv history, attention's per-row partition) all held.

**Memory and what allocates.** A fork copies into caches that admission allocated: nothing allocates
in the step path. The snapshot reserved for the store is allocated at admission (its exact size is
`KvCache::snapshot_bytes(p)`: `p` positions of K/V plus the whole recurrent state — about 20 MB for a
2B hybrid, whatever `p` is, plus K/V rows). Snapshots in the store are bounded by its byte budget
(`--prefix-cache-mb`, 768 MB by default); on reap, a group follower's full prompt is not stored for a
hybrid model, because its 20 MB state would only evict the shared beginning the fork kept. The fork
itself is `p` positions of K/V plus one copy of the recurrent state per follower — the price the
store already pays for a restore.

**What is left out** (part 2, S4.9j's second half): the chunked Gated DeltaNet scan
(`docs/design/s3.5-delta-scan.md`), which is what makes a *generic* (non-fork) prefix snapshot of a
hybrid cache restorable at arbitrary positions; the fork here is exact because it is taken where the
state is current.

## 7. S4.9f as built: the float GEMM

The kernel of decision 1, as it stands in the tree. Everything here is measured on the Ryzen AI 9
HX 370 (12 cores, AVX2 + AVX-512 F/BW/VL/VNNI/GFNI/VBMI, 1 MiB L2 per core, ~2 GHz under WSL2);
the micro-benchmark is `cargo run --release -p peregrine-bench --example fpbench`, the end-to-end
harness is `peregrine bench` and `/v1/embeddings` on Qwen3-Embedding 0.6B f16.

### Layout

A matrix `[n_out][n_in]` is cut into panels of 16 consecutive output rows; a panel is `n_in` steps
of 16 contiguous values, step `k` holding `W[r0..r0+16][k]`, in the file's own element type (f32
words, f16 halves or bf16 halves). The last panel is zero-padded. The loader reads each matrix in
64 MiB chunks and writes the panels into a store of its own (`FpStore`, the `TileStore` shape:
anonymous mapping, huge pages), and `TensorInfo::fp_off` points at them; the matrices' mapped pages
are therefore never faulted in — the same rule the k-quant repack follows. The token embedding is
not packed: it is gathered row by row, and when it is the tied output head the packed panels would
be a second full copy (311 MB on the 0.6B f16 file) for a matmul whose decode step is bound by those
bytes, not by the multiplies.

### The chain

Every output element is

```
acc = bias[r] (or +0.0); for k in 0..n_in { acc = fma(x[k], w[r][k], acc) }
```

one sequential FMA chain over the input index. The vector kernels compute exactly that chain, lane
per output, so the bits of a result do not depend on the tile, the tail, the row block, the thread
split or the vector width: `fpack::gemm_scalar` (the same chain with `f32::mul_add`, and the scalar
tier's kernel), AVX2 and AVX-512 agree bit for bit, and the tests assert `to_bits()` equality over
n_out 1..40, 1..40 activation rows, n_in 1..1024 including non-multiples of 16, with and without a
bias, and with inf, NaN and denormals. F16 is widened by `vcvtph2ps` and BF16 by shifting into the
high half; both are exact. (NaN *payloads* are the one thing a hardware FMA and a libm `fmaf` may
disagree on — the value class agrees, the payload need not. Real weights carry no NaN.)

The engine's `blocks::linear` drives a float group in units of **two panels × one L2-sized block of
activation rows** (`L2/2 / (4·n_in)`, at least one, read once from
`/sys/devices/system/cpu/cpu0/cache/index2/size` with 1 MiB as the default), partitioned by the same
dynamic counter or static share as every other path. The units are exactly the work the kernel would
do serially — a panel is re-read once per block either way — so splitting them changes no work, no
weight traffic and no bit.

### Shapes, and what the numbers chose

| shape | note | one-thread GMAC/s (1024×1024) |
|---|---|---|
| AVX2 6 rows × 16 outputs (12 ymm accumulators) | 1 panel per pass | 26.5-27.5 |
| AVX-512 12 rows × 32 outputs (24 zmm accumulators) | 2 panels per pass | **13.5** |
| AVX-512 2 × (6 rows × 16 outputs) (24 ymm accumulators) | 2 panels per pass, EVEX registers | **26.5-27.5** |

This CPU executes a 512-bit FMA as two 256-bit halves and issues one of them every other cycle, so
the zmm tile computes in the same time as two ymm tiles and pays twice the register pressure: it
measured half the throughput of the AVX2 tile. The AVX-512 tier therefore runs 256-bit arithmetic
with the extra sixteen registers, two panels in flight, which halves the activation broadcasts per
multiply. The single-row path (decode) is a GEMV of four panels in flight on AVX2; the eight-panel

### Bias

The kernel takes an optional starting accumulator (one value per output row of the run) and the
tests cover it, but the engine passes `None`: every model in the tree adds its biases *outside* the
matmul (`gpt-oss` adds `attn_output.bias` to the projection's output), and folding them into the
chain would change those models' bits for no accuracy gain. The BERT family (S4.6) can start the
chain from the bias when its linear layer wants it, and then it will be exact from the first row.

### The pass size: why an F16 file still trailed llama.cpp

An F16 file loses nothing to `vcvtph2ps`, so "lossy activations" could not be the whole of the
1.29-1.33× above. The remaining hypothesis was the **pass**: `prefill_chunk` rows per forward pass
streams the whole packed store once per 16 rows — 840 MiB at chunk 16, 16 passes/s at 250 tok/s, about
14 GB/s over the pass and 19 GB/s inside the matmul (below) — and llama.cpp forwards up to its whole
batch. Measured, 12 threads, `/v1/embeddings`, one

| 32 | 26.3 MiB | 166 MiB | 256.2 | 256.2 |
| 64 | 13.1 MiB | 199 MiB | 282.8 | 279.9 (0.64) |
| 128 (the cap) | 6.6 MiB | 277 MiB | **290.1** | **283.9** (0.65) |

**256 and 512 cannot be measured.** `PEREGRINE_PREFILL_CHUNK` is validated against

its default (16 at 12 threads — the option reads back as `default`). Lifting that cap is S4.9b's
token-budget pass, not this step's, and the default chunk is unchanged here.

**The hypothesis held, and it is worth 14 %.** At chunk 16 the f16 matmul times (46.3 ms per pass in

streaming read of a 1 GiB buffer on this machine: the pass was at ~90 % of the machine's bandwidth,
i.e. weight traffic and not the kernel. The Q8_0 file, at 446 MiB per pass, is under that ceiling at
every chunk and does not move (330.7 → 334.7, inside the ±3 % run-to-run band, and its 32 at chunk
32 is the band, not a regression). A bigger pass is what buys the f16 file its 14 %; on Q8_0 there is
nothing to buy.

**At equal pass size the two engines are level, and llama.cpp's remainder is its 512-row ubatch.**

### Cost

The store holds one copy of every packed float matrix in the file's own element type: 840 MiB for
the 1.2 GB f16 file (the token embedding is not packed). Resident memory on a one-text `embed`:
942 MB → 1116 MB (**+18 %**). Load time: 0.28-0.36 s → 1.3-4.1 s (median about 2 s; of it ~1.2 s is
the panel copy, the rest the file reads and first touch of the store). The store is rebuilt on every
start until S4.9e caches it beside the model, exactly as the k-quant repack is — so the 1.3-4.1 s and
the +18 % are paid on every run today, and S4.9e is what removes the first of them; that step's cache
key has to cover `PEREGRINE_FPACK`.

## 8. S4.9b as built (token-budget passes and prompt-sized caches)

**The budget.** A non-decoding pass fills up to `PEREGRINE_PASS_ROWS` rows with pooled (embedding)
and label (decision) slots, taking whole short texts and pieces of a long one from as many waiting
slots as fit. The default is two measured bounds, the smaller wins: half the L2 of activation rows
(`L2/2 / (4 × d_model)`; the design's `threads ×` factor overcounts buffers the workers share) and
the ridge where a pass turns compute-bound, `bytes_per_param × 10.8` (`FLOP/s ÷ (2 × bytes/s)`).
That is 16 rows for Q8_0, 22 for F16 and 32 for a bidirectional encoder. The bluefin sweep (16-512
rows, both models, §"bluefin-embed-2026-11-11") pinned it: the causal 0.6B Q8_0 file does
417 GFLOP/s in a 16-row pass and 372 at 256, so packing more rows only loses (7-16% at 32 and
above), while the 300M encoder is flat 32-512 and 12-25% ahead of 16. The matmul driver splits a
batch wider than its 128-row kernel scratch into blocks, so the budget is not capped by it; a row's
result does not depend on the block. A bidirectional encoder packs whole texts (never pieces) up to
the same budget, and a text longer than it runs alone.

**Admission.** Chat slots are bounded by `--slots`; embedding texts and decision questions are
bounded by the memory of their own prompt-sized caches (`PEREGRINE_WORK_CACHE_MB`, 512 MiB) and a
1 024-slot cap. The server scans its queue instead of taking it head-first, so a chat request waiting
for a slot never holds an embedding batch behind it, and a batch wider than the memory budget leaves
only its tail queued. Whatever is queued when the engine is free runs together; there is no timer.
A 128-text request on a `--slots 2` server is admitted whole and returns 128 vectors in input order,
each bit-identical to the text alone.

**Memory.** A pooled or label slot's cache — and each forked follower's — is its own prompt, rounded
up to whole 16-token prefill chunks. On Qwen3-Embedding 0.6B (112 KiB per position) a 16-token text
maps 1.75 MiB where it mapped the 896 MiB context before; a 113-token question maps 14 MiB. A chat
slot keeps the context it may decode into.

**Chat latency.** When a pass also carries decode rows, embedding work in it is bounded to a quarter
of a prefill chunk. Measured on Qwen3-Embedding 0.6B, `--slots 2`, beside two embedding streams: the
chat's 55.2 tok/s becomes 38.9 (1.4× lower); one prefill chunk of work would have made it 17.1
(3.2×) and `main`'s slot-blocked scheduler 7.5 (7.4×).

**What allocates.** Admission allocates, as it always has: the slot's cache (now the prompt's
positions instead of the context), mean pooling's f64 accumulator, and — for label slots — the
logits rows the pass will read (one per label slot plus the chat rows it may share a pass with, so
a step never grows them). The step path allocates nothing new: it still uses the engine's reused
`tokens`/`positions`/`map` vectors, and the matmul's row blocks write into the same scratch.
`crates/peregrine-models/tests/no_alloc.rs` stays green.

**Invariance.** A text's vector and a question's label logits are bit-identical alone, in any batch,
for budgets 1, 16, 64, 256 and the default, at 1, 2 and 4 threads, with prefill chunks 1 and 16,
beside chat slots, and through group forks (groups of 3 and 10). The existing goldens are unchanged.

## 9. S4.9g as built (the encoder attention strip)

**The layout.** For each KV head and each text of the pass, `peregrine_kernels::bidir::key_panel`
transposes the head's keys once into a panel `[head_dim][n]` — one row per head dimension, the
text's keys along the lanes — so a vector lane holds one *score* and the head dimension is folded
*inside* the lane. With GQA the panel is built once per KV head and serves its whole query group
(EmbeddingGemma: three query heads per key head). The values are not transposed: the mix reads the
pass's value rows in place, six query rows at a time, sharing one value load (§ part 2, below).

**The strips and the blocks.** `blocks/bidir.rs` builds the panels for one KV head in one
fork-join (a copy, not arithmetic: below 32 Ki copies it runs on the calling thread, because a
second fork-join costs more than it saves on a 16-token text), then walks the pass's rows with
`for_row_chunks`, re-chunking each worker's range by text and by strip. A strip is up to
`STRIP_MAX` = 32 query rows, narrowed to `STRIP_AREA / n` so `strip × n` f32 of scores stay in
`STRIP_AREA` = 32 Ki floats: 32 rows (64 KiB of scores, L2) at 512 tokens, 16 rows (128 KiB) at
2 000, 16 rows (1 KiB) for a 16-token text — one strip for the whole short text. A pass with fewer
strips than workers halves the strip down to `STRIP_MIN` = 16 so one long text still fills the pool,
and never below that (a short text is not cut up). Within a strip the keys are scored in blocks of
`SCORE_BLOCK` = 128 keys: that block's panel slice is `head_dim × 128 × 4` = 128 KiB at a 256-wide
head — a quarter of this machine's L2 — so what a row of the strip re-walks is bounded and L2-sized
rather than the whole 512 KiB panel. Measured, the block itself changes the score rate by under 1 %
at n = 512 (7.8 GFLOP/s either way): the cost there is the *stride* of the panel read, not the
slice's residency (see "what is left").

**The defined arithmetic, and its order.** For a text of `n` keys and query row `i` with window
`lo..hi = band(i, n, half)` (`half = n_swa/2` on a sliding layer, the whole text on a global one):

```text
score[j] = (fold k = 0..head_dim: acc = fma(q[k], key[k], acc)) * scale
max      = max(score[j])                                    j in lo..hi
e[j]     = exp(score[j] - max)                              j in lo..hi
l        = fold j ascending: sum += e[j] as f64             j in lo..hi
out[d]   = (fold j ascending: acc = fma(e[j], v[j][d], acc)) * (1/l)
```

`exp` is `vmath::exp8` on AVX2 and `vmath::exp_scalar` elsewhere — the same polynomial and the same
order, pinned equal on every input by `vmath`'s own test. The band is exact: a sliding layer
computes only the keys inside each row's window (the 8-lane tail of a window's end recomputes up to
seven keys of the same window, which is the vector width, not a mask). `scale` is 1.0 on this
family, which folds `1/√head_dim` into the queries as llama.cpp does. Everything is a fixed fold,
so a row's bits are a function of its own text, position and layer only — never of the strip it
fell in (`tests` hold strips of 1, 7, 16, 32 and `n` equal), the strip size, the thread, the other
texts of the pass or the kernel tier. The scalar twin is the reference: `tests/support/embed_ref.rs`
reimplements exactly these folds, and the engine-vs-reference test is bit-for-bit.

**What it costs, in memory.** `AttnScratch::bidir` holds the panel (`max_batch × head_dim` f32 —
one `[head_dim][len]` region per text at offset `row0 × head_dim`, because a pass's texts have
`max_batch` rows in total: 2 MiB for a 2 048-row pass of a 256-wide head) and one `STRIP_SLOT`
(32 Ki + 32 floats, 128 KiB) of scores and denominators per worker: 3 MiB at 8 threads. It is
sized in `Model::new_state` and in the engine's bidirectional branch, so the pass still allocates
nothing (the no-allocation test covers `forward_texts`).

### The score tile (part 2)

**Why a tile.** The strip above scored one query row at a time: four `ymm` of keys, one broadcast,
and therefore one panel run per twelve fused multiply-adds at best — the panel read, not the
arithmetic, held it at ~15 % of the FMA peak. But a score is a matrix-product element,
`score[i][j] = fold k: fma(q_i[k], key_j[k], acc)`, and every `(i, j)` keeps its own chain, which
is exactly the broadcast form of the float GEMM (§ S4.9f's 6×16 tile). `scores_tile` takes
`TILE_ROWS` = 6 query rows × `TILE_KEYS` = 16 keys (two `ymm`, twelve accumulators): per
head-dimension step the panel's sixteen keys load once and six query values broadcast into their
rows' accumulators, each lane still one `(i, j)` folded in head-dimension order. It is a
scheduling change only — no golden moved — and the tests pin tile shapes 1×8, 1×16, 2×16, 4×16,
6×16, 8×16, 6×24 and 4×32 to the scalar twin, and the tile to the row kernel.

**The band.** Rows of a strip have overlapping windows. Splitting a key block by the rows that
share it costs more than it saves, so the caller hands the tile the *union* of the strip's bands
for the block and the kernel scores all of it for every row: a key outside a row's band lands in
that row's buffer and is never read — the maximum, the exponentials and the mix all take the
row's own `band` — so the union is a scheduling choice, not a mask. At `STRIP_MAX` = 32 rows and
a 512-key window it adds 31 keys to a 512-key row (6 %); on a global layer it is the text.

**The shapes measured** (one thread, score kernel only, `Mpairs/s` and % of the 44.7 GMAC/s this
core reaches with twelve independent `ymm` chains; medians of three `bidirbench` runs at load
average 3.9-7):

| head_dim | n | row kernel (before) | 4×16 | 6×16 | 8×16 | 6×24 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 256 | 16 | 17.5 (10 %) | 99.3 (57 %) | **107.0 (61 %)** | 97.9 (56 %) | 108.4 (62 %) |
| 256 | 128 | 38.0 (22 %) | 85.5 (49 %) | **95.1 (55 %)** | 89.2 (51 %) | 69.4 (40 %) |
| 256 | 512 | 16.8 (10 %) | 45.7 (26 %) | **50.8 (29 %)** | 52.1 (30 %) | 43.1 (25 %) |
| 256 | 2 048 | 13.4 (8 %) | 36.4 (21 %) | 46.9 (27 %) | **53.1 (30 %)** | 41.0 (24 %) |
| 64 | 128 | 178.2 (26 %) | 313.8 (45 %) | **392.5 (56 %)** | 370.8 (53 %) | 288.6 (41 %) |
| 64 | 2 048 | 54.8 (8 %) | 156.7 (23 %) | 188.5 (27 %) | **199.8 (29 %)** | 169.3 (24 %) |
| 32 | 512 | 219.5 (16 %) | 521.1 (37 %) | **590.8 (42 %)** | 582.8 (42 %) | 505.0 (36 %) |

6×16 is chosen: twelve accumulators leave room for the two panel loads and the broadcast on

tie that the short and mid lengths break. 6×24 spills (eighteen accumulators on sixteen
registers). A key tile of 64 or 128 keys (the block) measures the same, so `SCORE_BLOCK` stays
128. An eight-row explicit prefetch of the panel row is worth ~15 % at 2 048 tokens (the rows
are 8 KiB apart there, past what the hardware prefetcher follows) and nothing at 128.

**The maximum and the mix.** `max_row` folds the scores with `maxps` instead of a four-cycle
`f32::max` chain; a maximum is exact, so nothing changed but the cost. The mix now shares one
value load between six rows (two `ymm` each) instead of four. Twelve rows of one `ymm` halve the
value traffic — the stage is L2/L3-resident at long texts — but double the broadcasts per
multiply-add: measured within noise of 6×2 at 2 048 tokens and ~10 % worse at 512, so 6×2 stays
(and the twelve-row path is kept, tested, as that measurement's control).

**Where the time goes now** (one thread, head_dim 256, share of the attention stage at

**What is left.** The score kernel reaches 55-61 % of the peak while the text fits in L2 and
28-29 % at 2 048 tokens; there, each strip re-reads a 128-key panel slice (128 KiB at head 256)
and the stride of the panel read (`n × 4` bytes per head dimension) is what the prefetch only

per head), which is why it reaches ~45 % of the peak at 128 tokens and ~25 % at 2 048; more rows
per load need a narrower head-dimension block and lose more to broadcasts than they save. AVX-512
has no kernel of its own here: the AVX2 one is already bit-identical across tiers, so the 512-bit
version is a pure throughput question (S4.9f's conventions apply).

### Long texts (part 3)

**What llama.cpp does.** Its tiled flash attention (`ggml_compute_forward_flash_attn_ext_tiled`
and `simd_gemm` in llama.cpp v0.9.0 `cd959ba`, `ggml/src/ggml-cpu/`) takes a 64-row query
block and a 64-key block at a time. Per (query block, key block) it copies the K rows into a
`K_f32[dk][kv]` tile — the 64 keys contiguous — runs a 6×2 register-blocked float GEMM for
`Q·Kᵀ`, scales it, updates a running row maximum (online softmax) and exponentiates the
64×64 score tile, copies the V rows into a contiguous 64-row tile and runs a second 6×2 GEMM
into the row's output accumulator. The only scores that ever exist are one 64×64 block's; K
and V rows are read once per 64-row query block; no `n × n` matrix is materialised. Its
arithmetic is not Peregrine's: the online maximum rescales the output accumulator by
`exp(m_old − m_new)` when a later key is larger, which is a different fold from "the row's
maximum, then every exponential".

Peregrine's strip had three long-text costs, all measured in part 2: the panel was
`[head_dim][n]`, so a score tile read one 64-byte run every `n × 4` bytes (27 % of the FMA
peak at 2 048); the value mix held six rows' accumulators, so a strip re-read the text's
value rows once per six-row block (51 % of the stage at 2 048); and the strip was 16 rows at
2 048, so the head's whole panel was read 125 times.

**What was tried, and what each gave.**

1. *A key-major tiled panel.* Built. On x86_64 a head's keys are stored in 16-key tiles —
   `panel[(j/16)·head_dim·16 + k·16 + j%16]` — so a score tile's head-dimension step reads
   the tile's sixteen keys as one contiguous 64-byte run, and consecutive steps are
   consecutive bytes. The last `n % 16` keys are one row-major region (no padding). The panel
   build is split by tile across the workers, so each tile has one writer. This is the change
   that took the score kernel from 26-49 % to 58-77 % of the peak.
2. *A key-blocked value mix.* Built. A strip now walks its keys in blocks of
   `MIX_KEY_BLOCK` = 128 for every row block of the strip before moving on, so a block's
   value rows stay in L1/L2 and the text's values are read from L3 once per strip rather than
   once per six-row block. Head dimensions are outside the row-block loop, so one
   accumulator pair's value slice (128 keys × 16 lanes × 4 = 8 KiB) is L1-resident across
   every row block. The accumulator lives in the output rows between blocks; inside a block
   the low edge, the shared core and the high edge still visit each row's keys in ascending
   order, so this is a scheduling change, not a reassociation.
3. *Fusing the score, the exponentials and the mix per key block.* Rejected. llama.cpp can
   fuse because its online softmax is allowed to rescale the accumulator; Peregrine's
   definition needs the row's exact maximum before the first exponential, so a block cannot
   be mixed before the row has been scored to its end. The bit-keeping version is two passes
   over the keys — maximum first, then score again, exponentiate and mix per block — which
   computes every score twice. Measured against the score share at 2 048 that is about +36 %
   of the stage, more than the strip buffer it would save. The `strip × n` scores stay.
4. *Strip height and the thread split.* A strip is now up to 64 rows and
   `STRIP_AREA` = 256 Ki floats (1 MiB per worker): 64 rows at 512-4 096 tokens, 32 at
   8 192, 8 at 32 768. Against part 2's 32 rows it is 1.2-1.3× the whole stage at 512-2 048.
   A 512 Ki area (64 rows at 8 192) is another 5 % at 8 192 and was not kept. The worker
   split is still (text, strip) units plus the panel's tiles; one long text fills four
   workers from 2 048 rows up.

## 10. S4.9e as built (the pack cache)

**What is cached.** The two load-time stores, together in one file: the k-quant store (`kq.rs`:
Q4_K, Q6_K, Q5_0 and Q8_0 into eight-row tiles) and the float store (`fpack.rs`: F32, F16 and BF16
into sixteen-row panels). The ternary PQ2_0 `TileStore` is built inside the qwen35 family's own load
and is not in this cache (see "Left out" below).

**The file.** A fixed header then the two stores at 4 KiB-aligned offsets:

| field | bytes | |
|---|---|---|
| magic | 8 | `PRGPACK\0` |
| version | 4 | `FORMAT_VERSION` (1 now), bumped with either layout |
| flags | 4 | which stores are present |
| key length, header length | 4 + 4 | |
| kq length, fp length | 8 + 8 | |
| kq offset, fp offset | 8 + 8 | page-aligned |
| model size, mtime (s, ns) | 8 + 8 + 4 + 4 | the file identity |
| GGUF header FNV | 8 | names, shapes, formats and offsets |
| body checksum | 8 | 64 sampled 4 KiB chunks |
| key | n | the canonical key text |
| header checksum | 8 | FNV-1a of everything above |

The body checksum samples 64 evenly spaced 4 KiB chunks including the first and the last, so a
truncated or badly corrupted body is rejected without hashing gigabytes; the length must also equal
the header's arithmetic. A foreign file is rejected by the magic, the header checksum, or the key.

**The key.** The model's size, mtime (seconds and nanoseconds) and the FNV-1a of the GGUF header
plus tensor directory, and the FNV-1a of a fixed sample of the weights (64 windows of 4 KiB spread
over the data section: 256 KiB read whatever the file's size, so a load never hashes the whole
file); `FORMAT_VERSION`; every packed tensor in store order with name, format, shape, offset and length;
the two repack switches; the kernel tier; the layout constants (8 tile rows, 16-row panels,
little-endian). A mismatch is a miss: the store is packed again and a new file written.

**Invalidation.** Touching the model changes its mtime; replacing it changes the header hash, and
a file with the same tensors, size and timestamp but other weights (a fine-tune copied over the old
file with its mtime kept) changes the weight sample. A
different `PEREGRINE_ISA` (or a build without the packed kernel) changes the tier or the switch.
Bumping a layout constant without `FORMAT_VERSION` is a bug the version is there to prevent.
`PEREGRINE_KREPACK`, `PEREGRINE_FPACK`, the options that choose the stores, and the tensors' own
formats are all in the key, so an A/B of either pack always packs rather than reading the other
layout.

**Concurrency and failure.** Writes go to `.tmp-<pid>-<hash>-<nanos>` in the same directory and are
renamed into place: a reader sees a complete old or new file, a crash leaves no usable file, and two
processes may pack the same model at once (both write, one rename wins, both load). An unwritable
directory logs one line and packs in memory; a cache file that fails any check is packed over
afterwards, never trusted. The directory cap (`PEREGRINE_PACK_CACHE_MB`, default 4096 MiB; `0`
disables eviction) is enforced after a write by mtime, least-recently-used first, skipping the file
just written; a hit updates the file's mtime so the order is an access order.

**Huge pages and first touch.** The mapped stores ask for `MADV_HUGEPAGE` when the in-memory stores
would (`PEREGRINE_TILE_HUGEPAGES`, default on), but this host's ext4 file mapping reports
`FilePmdMapped: 0 kB` and `/sys/kernel/mm/transparent_hugepage/shmem_enabled` is `never`: file-backed
THP is not available here, so the hint is a no-op. The mapping is also made with `MAP_POPULATE`,
which establishes the page tables at load; without it the first prefill paid one minor fault per
4 KiB and measured about 12 % slower prefill than the already-resident in-memory store. After it,
an alternating same-process comparison on SmolLM2-360M Q4_K_M (3 rounds, 4 threads) measured
69.7-87.9 tok/s prefill and 15.9-16.6 tok/s decode from the mapped cache against 62.2-74.4 and
16.0-16.7 in memory — within host noise, no slowdown.

**Measured (H0, Xeon E5-2680 v4, 8 vCPU; three runs, medians; the host was shared and its load
average moved between 8 and 17 during the session).** Load is process start to `model loaded`,
timed by a tool that only calls `peregrine_models::open`; one-shot is total wall time of the CLI
command. Cold = `PEREGRINE_PACK_CACHE=0` (the old path), first = an empty cache (pack and write),
warm = the cache present with the page cache warm.

| model | command | cold | first | warm | load speedup |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SmolLM2-360M-Instruct Q4_K_M | load | 0.304 s | 0.487 s | **0.062 s** | 4.9× |
| SmolLM2-360M-Instruct Q4_K_M | `run --max-tokens 8` | 4.83 s | 5.08 s | **2.73 s** | |
| Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B Q8_0 | load | 0.762 s | 1.367 s | **0.205 s** | 3.7× |

**Left out.** The ternary PQ2_0 `TileStore` (only qwen35 builds it, inside `Family::load`; it would
need the family's layout to be computed before load so the offsets could be set from the cache —
the k-quant store is the measured 1.7-22 s repack). A whole-body hash (the sampled check is the
cheap integrity check the step asks for; a stronger one is a format version away). Windows (not a
target). Pack caches for LittleBit sidecars. File-backed huge pages: unavailable here, measured
rather than assumed.

## 11. S4.9k as built: small work, the exact result cache, and why threading changed little

The step's list was: split by the shape of the work (output rows for one short text, whole texts
per thread for a batch), a measured no-fan-out threshold, one fork-join per layer for batches,
tokenise off the engine thread, and a cache keyed by token ids. The Part 1 profile (section
"Results, 4 threads" in `benchmarks/results/bluefin-2026-11-11-s4.9-standings/`) says which of
those have room; this section records what was built, what was measured and dropped, and why.

### What the profile said

On bluefin, Qwen3-Embedding 0.6B Q8_0, one 16-token text, the engine already scales with the
pool almost linearly:

| threads | texts/s | wall ms |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6.15 | 162.5 |
| 2 | 11.87 | 84.2 |
| 4 | 22.32 | 44.8 |

Even one short text fills four workers: the pass splits the *output rows*, and every worker reads
only its own weight rows. There is no row count below which fanning out loses on this shape. The
barrier wait is 1.5-2.9 % of a causal pass and 1.3-2.3 % of an encoder pass at 16 tokens; the
matmul is 80-90 %. A change that saves fork-joins therefore has a ceiling of a few percent, while
a change that turns repeated work into no work has no such ceiling.

### The exact result cache

`POST /v1/embeddings` now keeps finished vectors in a byte-bounded cache
(`crates/peregrine-server/src/embed_cache.rs`):

- **Key**: the token ids, the pooling and the requested `dimensions` — everything the returned
  vector is a function of. The model is fixed per process.
- **Value**: the *finished* vector (Matryoshka truncation and L2 normalisation applied), so a hit
  is byte-for-byte what a computation returns.
- **Bound**: `PEREGRINE_EMBED_CACHE_MB`, default **64 MiB**, `0` disables. Keys, vectors and a
  64-byte entry overhead all count. Eviction is insertion order (the oldest entry first).
  64 MiB holds about 16 300 vectors of 1024 f32 (4 KiB each) or 21 700 of 768 f32 (3 KiB each).
- **A batch's duplicates**: the request is planned before it is submitted, so a text that appears
  twice becomes one job and both positions take the same vector. Usage still counts every input.
- **Concurrency**: one `Mutex` around the map and its order; a lookup and an insert are short and
  no lock is held while the engine computes. Two requests that miss the same text may both compute
  it — the engine's invariance makes both vectors identical, so the second insert is harmless.
- **Observability**: `/metrics` gains `peregrine_embed_cache_entries` and
  `peregrine_embed_cache_bytes`; `peregrine_option{name="embed_cache_mb"}` reports the setting.

Measured on EmbeddingGemma 300M Q8_0, 4 threads, one text repeated (`benchmarks/embed_cache.py`,
three rounds, medians), cache off → 64 MiB:

| text tokens | one request | 32 identical texts |
|---|---:|---:|
| 16 | 12.62 → **0.74 ms** (17x) | 23.8 → **11.5 ms** (2.1x) |
| 128 | 98.85 → **1.16 ms** (85x) | 115.4 → **17.7 ms** (6.5x) |
| 512 | 489.5 → **2.22 ms** (220x) | 548.2 → **43.8 ms** (12.5x) |

The batch rows stop at the HTTP/JSON cost of returning 32 vectors (11.5 ms at 16 tokens), which the
engine no longer pays; the single-request rows are the cache's own claim. On the Part 1 grid, whose
texts are all distinct, the cache is neutral (the L16_b32/L128_b32 cells are within the ±3 %
run-to-run band); it is a serving win for repeated queries, not a throughput change for unique work.

### What was measured and not kept

- **No-fan-out threshold.** Tested at two levels. The pass level has none (the table above). A
  single short text's encoder attention is one strip of work, so fanning it out is a fork-join per
  KV head that no worker gains from; a threshold of 64 rows (serial for one 16-32-token text,
  parallel for a batch) measured 11.3 → 11.4 ms on EmbeddingGemma L16_b1 — no change. Not kept: the
  profile does not support it, and an extra branch in the hot path is not free.
- **One fork-join per layer, and whole texts per thread for a batch.** A 16-token pass executes
  336 barriers over 28 layers (12 per layer: q/k/v, attention, wo, gate/up, down, two norms, the
  quantise fills, the residual adds); a batch of 32 short texts is 32 such passes at the Q8_0 ridge
  (16 rows), not one 512-row pass, because 256/512-row passes are 13-14 % slower (next section).
  Giving each worker a whole text would cut the barrier count 4x while keeping each text's weight
  traffic at one read (the current split has every worker read only its share of the rows), but
  the matmul is 88-90 % of the pass and already linear in the pool, so the ceiling is the 2-3 %
  barrier wait, and the change needs per-text state, scratch and activation buffers. Not done.
- **Tokenise off the engine thread.** It already is: `/v1/embeddings` tokenises in the async
  handler before it queues any `EngineMsg`, and `peregrine embed` does the same before it admits.
  Measured 0.08 ms per 16-token text and 4.5 ms per 2 000-token text on this host, 0.2 % of the
  shortest cell and 0.05 % of the longest. No code change.
- **Integer activations for encoders** are decision 2's separate step; left out here as planned.

### The 256/512-row oddity: what it is

S4.9b saw `PEREGRINE_PASS_ROWS` 256 and 512 slower than 16-64 for a causal Q8_0 model. Reproduced
here on Qwen3-Embedding 0.6B Q8_0, one pass per request, 4 threads (tokens/s):

| pass rows | L16 x 32 | L128 x 32 |
|---|---:|---:|
| 16 (the default) | **353.5** | **344.7** |
| 64 | 313.4 | 306.6 |
| 128 | 319.7 | 313.2 |
| 256 | 314.9 | 310.9 |
| 512 | 310.0 | 300.6 |

It is the **matmul**, not the pass machinery. The matmul's share of the pass rises from 88.5 % at
16 rows to 89.7 % at 512, and its absolute time per token rises 15.6 %: 2.503 → 2.894 ms. The other
stages (attention, quantise, barrier wait, norms) shrink to 10.3 % of a longer pass. A 128-row
block's activation working set is 128 x 1024 x 4 B = 512 KiB — this core's whole L2 — and the
weight stream evicts it continuously, while the eight-row Q8_0 tile re-reads that activation once
per eight output rows. At 16 rows it is 64 KiB and stays resident. A 128-row block split into
64-row blocks (a local, bit-neutral change tried for this report) measured 315.3 at pass rows 256
and 312.2 at 512 — no better, because the streaming weights still evict it. A real fix is a
cache-blocked loop order (weights x activation tiles) in the Q8_0 tile kernel, which is a kernel
step, not a small fix; the default already keeps a causal Q8_0 pass at 16 rows, and the encoder's
32-row default is flat from 32 to 512, so nothing shipped changed.

## 12. S4.9h as built: the bert family's element-wise work

**The kernels, and the defined result.** `peregrine-kernels::vmath` keeps the scalar
`erf`/`gelu_erf`/`layer_norm_row` definitions S4.6a wrote, and puts AVX2 bodies beside them, both
`to_bits` equal to their scalar twins:

- `gelu_erf_rows` evaluates sixteen elements per step, four `f64` lanes per `erf` (a lane is an
  element). The three Maclaurin polynomials the definition selects by `|x|` are three
  independent Horner chains, selected per lane. A sixteen-element block takes the shortest path
  all of its lanes can take — only the 10-term chain when every `|x/√2| ≤ 1`, the 10- and
  22-term chains when every `|x/√2| ≤ 2` — and the lanes on that path get exactly the arithmetic
  they would otherwise. The rare lane in `(3, 4.6)` calls the same scalar `erfc_tail`; `a ≥ 4.6`
  is exactly 1; a NaN keeps its own bits (the scalar `erf` returns its argument unchanged).
- `layer_norm_rows` normalises eight rows per step, one `f64` lane per row. Each row's mean and
  variance still accumulate in index order in one accumulator, and the mean, the scale and its
  rounding are the definition's. The scale-and-shift is eight `f32` lanes along the row with the
  same three unfused operations. The bits are equal for every finite row. A row holding an
  infinity or a NaN makes the same NaN on both paths, but its payload is not part of the
  definition: narrowing a NaN from `f64` to `f32` differs between `cvtsd2ss` and `vcvtpd2ps`.

The tests hold the two paths equal over 1 000 000 random bit patterns (every exponent, both
signs, subnormals, infinities and NaNs), the series' range boundaries and their neighbours, ±0,
and row lengths 1..40 for every remainder of the 16- and 8-wide bodies.

**On aarch64, as built.** NEON bodies evaluate the same sequences: eight GELU elements per
step with two `f64` lanes per `erf` (four `float64x2_t` groups), and eight LayerNorm rows per
step with one `float64x2_t` lane pair per two rows. Each `erf` lane still gets the definition's
Horner chain in the definition's order, and each LayerNorm row still accumulates into a single
`f64` in index order; the scale-and-shift is the same three unfused `f32` operations. The same
test holds the vector and scalar paths equal over the million random patterns, the range
boundaries, ±0, the infinities and NaNs, and widths 1..40.

Measured on the ARM host, all-MiniLM-L6-v2 F16 (10 threads, one process at a time, medians of
three processes): 16 tokens 571 → 591 texts/s, 128 tokens 114 → 114, 512 tokens 23.8 → 24.0.
The element-wise work is too small a share of these passes for the change to show outside the
host's noise: at 512 tokens the pass is 59 % matmul and 23 % attention after the change. The
same run's attention stage is 12 → 9 ms at 512 tokens, which is the S4.9g NEON tile, not this
section's work.

**What the pass no longer does.** A model whose every matrix is F32/F16/BF16 reads only the
`f32` activation rows. `QuantAct::fill` now keeps that copy and skips the int8 layouts
(`ActLayout::quantize`; the dense and gemma-embedding families set the same flag), which is the
S4.9a rule applied to a pure float file. The norms and the GELU fill the next consumer's
activation in the same pool task that produced the rows, and for a float file that fill is now a
copy of the row the kernel just read. The attention-output bias and residual are applied to each
matmul-sized chunk as the projection leaves it, so the residual walk is not a second pass; the
down projection's bias and residual are one fused pass; `bias_gelu_fill` holds the bias, the GELU
and the fill in one row task.

**Measured.** MiniLM-L6-v2 F16, one 420-token text, 4 threads, min of 12 warm passes on H0 (8
vCPU Xeon E5-2680 v4, AVX2, **shared host**: load average 6.6-6.8, other jobs running), base
commit against this branch:

| phase (ms, 420 tokens) | base | after |
|---|---|---|
| embedding gather | 1.27 | 1.24 |
| embedding norm + fill | 0.29 + 0.49 | 0.21 |
| q/k/v | 18.29 | 15.83 |
| attention | 48.15 | 27.9 |
| `attn_output` + bias + residual | 13.75 | 6.0 |
| `attn_output_norm` + fill | 2.07 + 4.24 | 0.6 |
| `ffn_up` | 25.37 | 20.7 |

The pass is 1.77× shorter and the element-wise work 3.9× shorter. GELU + LayerNorm + fills is
10.3 % of the pass here, against 22.7 % for the base commit measured the same way (the S4.6a

between the two runs (the base run's attention and matmuls were 1.2-1.7× slower), so only the
element-wise rows are a like-for-like comparison; the interleaved text-per-second runs below are
the end-to-end measure. The host was oversubscribed: four "threads" shared the machine with about
fourteen runnable tasks, so the isolated kernel rate is the more stable number —
`gelu_erf_rows` runs at 2.5 ns/element on a 1 536-element row (previously the scalar twin's

On a 16-token text the fixed per-pass work dominates: GELU + LayerNorm + fill is 1.09 ms of a
6.9 ms pass (15.8 %), against 1.36 ms of 5.1 ms (26.8 %) on the base commit. Interleaved runs
(old and new alternating, medians of five) give 71.7 → 91.1 texts/s at 16 tokens (batch 1) and
107 → 122 (32 texts), 5.6 → 9.9 and 10.9 → 17.3 at 128 tokens, 2.0 → 2.5 and 2.8 → 4.1 at 512
tokens.

**Accuracy and tiers.** No golden changed. The scalar and vector bodies are `to_bits` equal, so
every F32/F16/Q8_0 vector is the base commit's: `peregrine embed` on the three downloaded files
(MiniLM F16, MiniLM Q8_0, bge-small Q8_0) prints the same bits for five texts of different
lengths before and after, and the 200-text real-file gate still prints the reference note's
numbers (MiniLM F16 vs the fp32 reference 0.9999996/0.9999998). `PEREGRINE_ISA=scalar` keeps the
F16 vectors identical to AVX2; the Q8_0 files differ across tiers exactly as they did on the base
commit (the int8 kernels' own tier arithmetic), and the scalar output is the base commit's scalar
output.

**What is left.** The GELU is now the largest element-wise item. Its per-lane term counts are
the definition's, so a shorter polynomial is a redefinition (S4.9h's second route, with the three
goldens re-recorded and the accuracy table re-measured), not an optimisation of the present one.
The `xf` copy a float file still makes is the packed-float GEMM's interface, not quantisation;
reading the source rows directly would remove it. AVX-512 has no body of its own: the AVX2 one is
already bit-identical across tiers, so that is a throughput question. Threading the element-wise
work better than the row split is S4.9k's.

## 13. S4.9m as built (part b): fewer passes over the activations on the float path

## 14. S4.9m as built (part a): whole texts in a pass, and why whole-slice threading was not kept

**The measurement.** With part (b) in, the pass was still handing a small encoder's batch to the
weights in pieces of 32 rows: `pass_rows_default_for`'s bidirectional floor. A 32-text batch of
16-token texts is 512 rows, so it ran **16 weight passes** over all-MiniLM's 44 MiB of weights;
each pass is bound by that stream, not by the 128-row block the packed kernel wants. The cell
scaled worse than the single text (543 against 622 direct texts/s before part (b)), and a
32-text batch of bge-small's 132 MiB of F32 weights was a 16-pass stream of 2.1 GiB.

A sweep over the budget (`PEREGRINE_PASS_ROWS` 32/64/128/256/512, the same cells, direct engine)
put the knee at **128 rows**, the packed kernel's own activation block: MiniLM F16 16 tok × 32
730 → 831 texts/s (64 and 128 equal), 16 tok × 8 747 → 803; bge-small F32 16 tok × 32 354 → 431,
16 tok × 8 335 → 417. The 128-token and 512-token cells were flat (the pass is already whole
texts there), and 256/512 rows were worse. A **Q8_0** encoder is the exception: its tile kernels'
activation working set outgrows L2 above 32 rows, and bge-m3 Q8_0 measured 3% slower at 128
(16 tok × 8 28.7 → 27.9 texts/s), so the quantised path keeps the 32-row floor.

**The change.** A bidirectional model whose every matrix is float (`act_layout().quantize` off)
and whose weight store is at most `SMALL_FLOAT_ENCODER_BYTES` (256 MiB) now takes whole-text
passes of 128 rows; a quantised model, and a float one past that size, keep the measured 32-row
floor. `Model::act_layout` exposes the layout the engine already builds for its scratch.
`PEREGRINE_PASS_ROWS` still overrides either. The pass size was always a scheduling parameter: a
text's vector does not depend on it, and the invariance tests pin budgets 1, 16, 64 and 256.

**Whole-slice threading: measured, not kept.** With (b) and this change the matrix stage is
98% busy inside the kernel (`PEREGRINE_PROF2=2`: e.g. 512 tokens × 1, 32.8 of 33.6 ms), the
barrier wait is 1.5-4.0% of the pass, and the dynamic row/panel partition is already balanced.
Handing a whole text (or a whole 128-row block) to one thread for a whole layer can only remove
the fork-join, which is that 1.5-4%, and the kernel runs at the same 43-46 GMAC/s per core. On
these shapes a matrix call is tens of microseconds, but the call structure is not what the pass
spends its time on; the weight stream and the kernel were. So this PR does the row-budget half of
part (a) and leaves the threading unchanged, as S4.9k's causal measurements also concluded.

## 15. S4.9m as built (part c): small Q8_0 encoders on the float path

**The idea (decision 2 of this design).** A Q8_0 weight is a one-scale-per-32 code: `f16 scale ×
int8 code`, which is exact in f32. A small Q8_0 encoder can therefore be dequantised **once at
load** into the packed float kernel's f32 panels and run on the float path like an F16 file: no
activation quantisation (S4.9a's serial fill was 15.5-17.5% of the Q8_0 bert pass), no int8 tile
store, and a vector closer to the fp32 reference. It costs four times the weight bytes, so it is a
small-model choice and has an off switch.

**The rule, measured.** The float path wins while the streamed weights are small enough that the
pass's compute pays for them. On bluefin, 4 threads, direct engine, Q8_0 file vs the same Q8_0
file dequantised to f32 panels (threshold lifted for the measurement), texts/s:

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|

| bge-small-en-v1.5 (34 MiB) | **316** / 281 | 322 / **417** | 39.1 / **50.8** | 37.1 / **50.4** |
| EmbeddingGemma 300M (330 MiB) | **89.7** / 63.3 | **92.0** / 77.6 | 11.01 / **11.17** | 11.10 / **11.16** |
| bge-m3 XLM-R (604 MiB) | **29.7** / 23.1 | **22.0** / 19.8 | 2.92 / **3.05** | 3.23 / **3.39** |

The two small `bert` files win from eight texts up and lose 7-11% on one 16-token text (the single
text streams the whole f32 store once for very little compute: MiniLM's panels are 88 MiB against
the 23 MiB file, bge-small's 136 MiB against 34 MiB). At 300M the float path is 29% slower at 16
tokens and level at 128; at 604 MiB it loses 22% at 16 tokens and gains 4-5% at 128. The loss
begins where the f32 stream stops being paid for by the pass's compute, so the rule is **64 MiB
of file weights** (256 MiB of f32 panels), where both measured small files win; EmbeddingGemma
300M and bge-m3 keep the int8 tiles. The same constant is the pass-budget rule above.
`PEREGRINE_Q8_FLOAT=0` keeps the int8 tiles for the small files too. (The rule is scoped to

**The change.** `fpack::layout_with(g, q8_as_f32)` adds every Q8_0 matrix of a `bert` file to the
float store with `FpEntry::kind = F32` and `source = Q8_0`; `repack_with` reads the file's Q8_0
rows, dequantises them (`scale × code`, exact), and writes f32 panels. The tensor keeps its file
format and gains `TensorInfo::fp_kind = Some(F32)`, so the float path (`panel_fmt`) reads the f32
panels while anything reading the file's own bytes (the token embedding, the reference forward)
sees Q8_0. The k-quant repack leaves those matrices out (`kq::layout_with`), and the model's
`act_layout` counts them as float, so the pass builds no int8 activation layout. The pack cache
now keys each float entry by both `kind` and `source` and `FORMAT_VERSION` is 2, so an old pack
can never be mistaken for the converted form.

## 16. S4.9n as built: the modern-bert element-wise kernels

`geglu_erf` and `layer_norm_rows_nobias` were the last two scalar kernels of the ModernBERT
family. Both get AVX2+FMA bodies that are `to_bits` equal to their scalar twins, so no golden
changed on any tier. `geglu_erf` runs sixteen elements per step through the same erf-GELU body as
`gelu_erf_rows` and then the one multiply by `up`; its tail goes through the scalar `gelu_erf`,
which is bit-identical to the vector `erf`. `layer_norm_rows_nobias` runs eight rows per step with
the bias form's eight-lane `f64` reduction and ends in one multiply by the weight and no add, so a
row of `-0.0` stays negative. The tests, not the goldens, carry the proof: lengths 0, 1, 7, 8, 9,
15, 16, 17, 31, 33, 768, 1152 and 2304; ±0, subnormals, ±1e30, ±inf and NaN (bits for everything
else, `is_nan` on both sides for NaN); a 200 000-value sweep of [-12, 12]; and for the norm several
batches, an all-`-0.0` row, a constant row, width 768 and widths that are not a multiple of eight.

Isolated, at ModernBERT-base's shape (512 tokens, 22 layers × 1152 gate elements, 45 × 768-wide
norm rows) on one thread of this machine, median of five runs:

| kernel | before | after | speedup |

## 17. S4.9o as built (2026-11-12): Q8_0 encoders on the int8 ARM tiers

**What ran before.** A Q8_0 encoder's linears are handed to `MatKernels::path`, which picks packed
float panels first, then the repacked eight-row tiles, then the per-row kernel. Which one a Q8_0
file got was decided by two things only: whether S4.9m's dequantise-at-load rule covered the file
(`architecture == "bert"` and at most 64 MiB), and whether the CPU has `sdot`. So on the ARM host:

| tier | `bert` (MiniLM, 22 MiB) | `modern-bert` (gte, 151 MiB) |
|---|---|---|
| `aarch64` | float panels (f32) | per-row `q8_0_batch`, int8 activations |
| `aarch64-neon` | float panels (f32) | per-row `q8_0_batch`, int8 activations |
| `aarch64-neon-dotprod` | float panels (f32) | repacked 8-row tile, `sdot`, int8 activations |
| `aarch64-neon-i8mm` | float panels (f32) | the same `sdot` tile — **Q8_0 has no `i8mm` body** |

Two facts explain the S4.9n note "Q8_0 is 12.3 texts/s where the same file in F16 is 20.3". The
`i8mm` tier is not an `i8mm` tier for a Q8_0 file: `q4k8_gemm_i8mm`, `q5_08_gemm_i8mm` and
`q6k8_gemm_i8mm` exist, `q8_08_gemm_i8mm` does not, so a Q8_0 matmul runs the dotprod tile. And
the F16 file of the same model takes the packed float panels, whose NEON body is a much stronger
kernel. llama.cpp's Q8_0 path is a `smmla`/`sdot` GEMM on the same weights, so it was 8-25 % ahead
at 128 tokens and up, and Peregrine's Q8_0 pass was 40 % behind its own F16 pass.

**Measured** (this host: DGX Spark, aarch64, 20 cores in two clusters; 10 threads pinned to the
ten Cortex-X925 cores `taskset -c 5-9,15-19`; seven interleaved rounds, medians, embedding cache
off; `benchmarks/results/spark-arm-2026-11-12-q8-encoders/`):

| gte-modernbert-base Q8_0 | 16 tok | 128 tok | 512 tok | 16 × 32 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `aarch64-neon-i8mm` (sdot tile) | 78.703 | 12.295 | 3.132 | 47.747 |
| `aarch64-neon-dotprod` (sdot tile) | 76.987 | 12.342 | 3.135 | 48.150 |
| `aarch64-neon` (per-row) | 25.678 | 3.094 | 0.800 | 13.655 |
| same file, F16, native | 110.582 | 20.747 | 5.333 | 76.436 |
| llama.cpp Q8_0 | 73.691 | 14.203 | 3.586 | 62.650 |

So forcing the lower tier does **not** help: the plain-neon route is the per-row kernel and it is
3.1-3.9× slower, because it quantises the activation row per projection and reads one weight row
per dot. What is faster is the float *weights* route, which the `bert` family already took on every
tier. A throwaway build with S4.9m's rule lifted for `modern-bert` (no code committed) measured
97.725 / 20.634 / 3.928 / 92.357 texts/s — faster than the sdot tile at every cell (+24 % at 16
tokens, +68 % at 128, +25 % at 512, +93 % on the batch). It is not a long-text-only effect, so no
shape-dependent switch is needed (and a row's bits may not depend on the batch or the length, so
there could not be one).

**The change.** On `aarch64-neon-dotprod` and up — the tiers that carry the Q8_0 int8 tiles — a
`modern-bert` file of at most `Q8_FLOAT_MAX_BYTES_ARM` (256 MiB) is dequantised once at load into
the float kernel's f32 panels, exactly as a small `bert` file already was: `fpack::layout_with`/
`repack_with` add every convertible Q8_0 matrix with `kind = F32` and `source = Q8_0`, the tensor
keeps its file format and gains `fp_kind = Some(F32)`, the k-quant repack leaves those matrices
out, and `act_layout().quantize` turns off because every linear now reads the f32 rows. The
constant is 256 MiB — `SMALL_FLOAT_ENCODER_BYTES`, so a converted file is still a "small float
encoder" for the pass-size rule and its panels stay under 1 GiB. The measured file is 151 MiB;
bge-m3 (604 MiB) keeps the tiles, and the Granite R2 Q8_0 files, not on this machine, are smaller
than the constant and would take it too. The scoping is `arch_name == "modern-bert" &&
cfg!(target_arch = "aarch64") && cpu::have_dotprod()`: the lower aarch64 tiers keep the per-row
kernel they already ran, and x86 keeps the int8 tiles and the 64 MiB `bert` rule (S4.9m measured
the float route losing past 64 MiB there, and only ARM was measured here). `PEREGRINE_Q8_FLOAT=0`
keeps the tiles for an A/B.

**Why it is exact.** `scale × code` is exact in f32, so the panels hold the file's weights with no
loss, and the float path quantises no activation: it is strictly more accurate than the int8 tile,
which rounds the activation row to int8 and folds the block scale into an int32 accumulator. The
synthetic Q8_0 file's vector is now the scalar float reference's **bit for bit** on both int8 tiers
— `embeddings.rs`'s modern-bert Q8_0 test asserts equality whenever the file took the float path,
and prints max abs 0.0 for every case. On the 200 real texts, against llama.cpp's F16 conversion,
min/mean cosine goes 0.9963540/0.9996125 → 0.9994969/0.9997834 (llama.cpp's own Q8_0:
0.9981270/0.9996284), and against llama.cpp's Q8_0 0.9969122/0.9998028 → 0.9983872/0.9998352.
Peregrine's Q8_0 is now closer to full precision than llama.cpp's Q8_0 is.

## 18. S4.9q as built (2026-11-12): the Q8_0 tile's `smmla` body

S4.9o's last line said the Q8_0 `i8mm` tile kernel "has no measured reason". That was true for
the `modern-bert` files it measured, which take the float route now, and false for the Q8_0 files
that stay on the int8 tile. `gemma-embedding` (EmbeddingGemma 300M), `qwen3-embed`,
`mistral3` (Nemotron 3.5 Embed 1B), bge-m3, and every `bert`/`modern-bert` file past
`Q8_FLOAT_MAX_BYTES*` still hand their Q8_0 matrices to `MatKernels::path`'s `KqTiles` arm, and on
`aarch64-neon-i8mm` that arm ran `q8_08_gemm` — the `sdot` body — because the format was the one
repacked tile without an `smmla` body.

**The body.** Q8_0's tile is eight rows of one block each: eight f16 scales and 256 signed codes,
laid out `qs[window * 64 + row * 8 + k]`, so a window is two output rows of eight codes — exactly
`smmla`'s first operand — and two activation rows' eight codes, joined, are its second (the
per-32 activation layout is the plain one, so the join is two eight-byte loads). One instruction
forms 32 products, four cover a window's four row pairs, and a block's four windows share one
scale and no offset, so they accumulate into one int32 sum with no per-window widening. The
epilogue is `kq::dot_q8_08_ref`'s `(d·dk)` rounded once and one fused multiply-add per block, in
the tile's order — the `sdot` body's expression in its order, so the result is bit-identical, not
merely within rounding. `q8_08_gemm_i8mm` runs whole groups of four activation rows and leaves
the 1–3-row tail to the `sdot` group; `q8_08_gemm_i8mm`'s GEMV sibling stays `sdot` (one
activation row cannot fill `smmla`'s second column, and the per-row bits are pinned by the same
tests). `MatKernels::kq_tiles_q8_0` picks the new body when `kq_i8mm`, which is `have_i8mm()`;
the dotprod tier's behaviour and goldens are untouched, and `kernels_for(Q8_0)` now lists
`neon-i8mm`.

## 19. S4.9p as built: the halved-precision float panel, widened once, and the roof measured (2026-11-12)


S4.9l left the two small BERT encoders at 0.85–0.99× of the best equal-accuracy engine at 128
and 512 tokens, with a 512-token pass that is 58–60 % matmul, 23–25 % attention and 16–17 %
"other". The obvious reading — "the matrix kernel is 15 % slow" — is wrong, and measuring the
machine is what shows it.

**The roof.** `telem::Machine::probe`'s twelve independent `ymm` chains reach 512.6 GFLOP/s on
4 threads and 64.9 GMAC/s (129.8 GFLOP/s) on one. Zen 2 retires 2 × 8 f32 FMA per cycle, so
129.8 GFLOP/s ÷ 32 FLOP/cycle = 4.06 GHz under load, and the 4-core roof is **259.6 GMAC/s**.

**The kernel and the rivals on the same cell.** MiniLM's 512-token pass is 5.436 GMAC of
matrix product (q, k, v, attention output at 384 × 384 and up/down at 384 × 1536, six layers);
bge-small's is 10.872 GMAC (twelve layers). Measured, 4 threads:

| | MiniLM f16 512 | bge-small f32 512 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Peregrine matmul | 32.94 ms = 165.0 GMAC/s (63.6 % of the roof) | 60.96 ms = 178.3 GMAC/s (68.7 %) |
| OpenVINO `FullyConnected`, its own profiler | 31.38 ms = 173.2 GMAC/s (66.7 %) | 62.59 ms = 173.7 GMAC/s (66.9 %) |
| Peregrine attention | 12.69 ms | 25.66 ms |
| OpenVINO attention (`Subgraph` + `Transpose`) | 11.14 ms | 20.69 ms |
| Peregrine element-wise, embedding, pooling | **9.26 ms** | **18.05 ms** |
| OpenVINO's (`MVN`, `Gather`, the rest) | **0.55 ms** | **1.14 ms** |
| pass total | 54.84 ms | 104.44 ms |

So the matmul is at parity with oneDNN's packed FMA kernel on this CPU (within 5 %, and ahead on
bge-small), and **the gap is the element-wise work**: 17 % of Peregrine's pass against 1 % of
OpenVINO's, because oneDNN fuses LayerNorm, GELU and the residual add into the GEMM epilogue
while this engine writes each of them as its own read-modify-write pass over the block. bge-small
loses a further 5 ms in the attention strip (32-wide heads leave the score tile a 32-step `k`
loop, and the strip is walked four times). Closing either is a different task; this one records
the split, because the next step should not start at the kernel again.

**What was changed anyway, and why.** The halved-precision tile *was* short of the roof, for a
reason that only shows in the disassembly: `gemm_avx2` carried the format `match` and the
prefetch guard inside the `k` loop, and f16/bf16 paid the widening on the two ports the twelve
FMAs use — fourteen vector operations per step where twelve would do (77 % of the roof against
f32's 91 %). Two changes, both in `fpack`:

- the format is a macro parameter, so `gemm_avx2_f32`, `_f16_direct` and `_bf16_direct` are
  three monomorphised kernels and the dispatch is one branch per call;
- a halved-precision matrix is widened **once per panel** into a per-thread f32 scratch
  (`WIDE`, 24–96 KiB, half of L2 at most) and then multiplied by the *f32* tile loop, so the
  conversion is paid `n_in · 16` times per (panel, row block) instead of once per row tile —
  21× less at a 128-row block. Below 18 activation rows (three tiles) the widening costs more
  than it saves and the direct loop runs instead.

The bits cannot move: the widening is exact (`vcvtph2ps`, `fcvtl`, the bf16 high-half shift),
every output is still one sequential `fma` chain over `k`, and the f32 loop is the same chain
with the same operands in the same order. New tests pin it: widened against direct on F16 and
BF16 for six shapes × rows 1…64, every row count 1–40 and 127/128/129/511/512/513 at ten
widths (including 37, 383, 385, 513, 1023, 1537), and ±0, subnormals, 1e-38, 2^-14, ±3e4,
±6.5e4, ±inf and NaN as activations and as weights. One thread, `fpbench`, 384 × 384 at 128
rows: f16 **50.2 → 57.3 GMAC/s** (+14 %), bf16 **51.5 → 57.2** (+11 %), f32 unchanged at 59.2;
on 2048 × 8192 (where the scratch does not fit and only the branch moved) f16 44.6 → 52.6
(+18 %).

**End to end**, three interleaved rounds per binary, medians (`proof_run.py`, same flags as
S4.9l):

| cell | before | after |
|---|---:|---:|
| MiniLM f16 L512 b1 / b8 / b32 | 0.87× / 0.88× / 0.89× | **0.90× / 0.91× / 0.94×** |

## 21. S4.9r as built: the encoder's element-wise block, threaded and batched (2026-11-12)

S4.9p's account left the two small BERT encoders behind OpenVINO in everything *around* the
matrix products: 9.26 ms of a 512-token MiniLM pass (18.05 on bge-small) against 0.55 / 1.14 ms,
and attention 12.69 against 11.14 (25.66 against 20.69). This section is the step-by-step account
of that block, and the three changes that close the part of it the arithmetic allows. The full
tables, the OpenVINO per-node types beside every step and the exact commands are in
`benchmarks/results/bluefin-2026-11-12-s4.9r/README.md`.

**The account** (temporary thread-local timers in `Bert::forward_texts`, not committed; medians
of four or five `cellprof` invocations, one text per pass, 4 threads). MiniLM 512: token
embedding 0.49, token-type add 0.03, position embedding 0.43, position add 0.03, embedding
LayerNorm+fill 0.29, copy 0.03, q/k/v biases 0.66, query scale 0.14, `wo` bias+residual 0.35,
attn LayerNorm+fill 0.36, **ffn_up bias+GELU+fill 4.48**, ffn_down bias+residual 0.35, out
LayerNorm+fill 0.41, pooling 0.04 — 8.64 ms of steps, plus 0.67 ms of thread wake-up across 187
fork-joins and bookkeeping. bge-small 512 (12 layers): 0.09/0.03/0.08/0.03/0.30/0.03, q/k/v
biases 1.44, scale 0.33, `wo` post 0.70, attn norm 0.74, **GELU 10.66**, ffn_down residual 0.67,
out norm 0.81 — 16.65 plus 1.13. Attention at 512: key panel 1.05, scores 2.98 (202 GMAC/s, at
the roof), row max 0.42, **exp + denominator 5.30**, mix 3.66 (168 GMAC/s). At 128 tokens every
step is a quarter of these and the two totals are 1.76 and 3.52 ms.

Two facts fell out. The GELU is `0.77 ns` — 3.1 cycles — per element: one sequential `f64`
Horner chain per lane (10, 22 or 36 terms by range, then a per-lane blend of the three
polynomials, because a lane in `(1, 2]` *is* the 22-term series). It is latency-bound on `f64`
FMA and cannot be shortened without changing a bit: per-group path selection measured 3.7 → 6.0
ns/element, and 32-lane blocks cannot shorten the 36-term chain either. And the softmax
denominator is one ascending `f64` sum per row, 512 dependent adds at four cycles each — 0.14 ns
per element, three of every four cycles deliberately idle. OpenVINO's `PERF_COUNT` shows the
structural difference on the other side: `Gelu` and the residual `Add`s are **0.00 ms** as nodes
because oneDNN fuses them into the `FullyConnected` post-ops; its surviving element-wise nodes
(MVN 0.40, Gather 0.20, Transpose 0.49, Convert 0.15, the non-attention `Subgraph` 1.09) total
2.36 ms on MiniLM and 3.76 on bge-small against Peregrine's 9.31 and 17.78.

**The changes.** (1) `peregrine_kernels::bidir::exp_rows4_sums`: four rows of one global layer's
strip, one shift each, `exp8` eight keys at a time, and the four ascending `f64` folds advanced
interleaved — four chains in flight instead of one, each row's bits untouched. Softmax 5.30 →
3.18 ms (MiniLM) and 10.50 → 6.46 (bge-small). (2) The q/k/v bias adds and the query scale, and
the two residual+bias reads after `attn_output` and `ffn_down`, run in one fork-join over rows
each instead of on the calling thread; the zero-bias skip is kept so a `-0.0` stays. 0.80 → 0.22
and 0.70 → 0.35 (MiniLM), 1.77 → 0.49 and 1.37 → 0.70 (bge-small). (3) F16 embedding rows widen
sixteen halves per step with `vcvtph2ps` instead of a scalar `f16` loop, with the scalar twin as
the definition and a NaN-payload fallback (F16C quiets NaN). 0.92 → 0.12 ms on MiniLM; bge-small
is F32 and did not move. The GELU's AVX2 body walks 32 elements per call instead of 16 (3.60 →
3.45 ms, 4 %).

**The bits.** Every new body is held to its scalar twin by tests: `exp_rows4_sums` against
`exp_row` + `sum_row` for every length 0–40 and 127/128/129/383/384/385/511/512/513, two strides,
random batches, ±0, the smallest subnormal, 1e-38, ±88, ±inf and NaN; the F16 widening against
`dequant_f16_scalar` over **all 65 536 half patterns** and every encoder width. End to end, a
512-token text's vector is the same 4 789 floats (MiniLM) and 4 721 (bge-small) before and after,
at 1 and 4 threads, alone and in a batch of 8; no golden file changed.

**End to end** (three interleaved rounds, medians, `proof_run.py`): MiniLM's F16 row 16.76 →
**20.40** texts/s at L512 b1 (+21.7 %), 18.70 → 20.64 at b8, 18.88 → 20.48 at b32, 95.7 → 107.0 on
the mixed batch, and 0.82× → **0.99×** / 0.91× → **1.00×** / 0.93× → **1.01×** against the best
equal-accuracy engine; the Q8_0 row (same path) 0.88× → **0.99×** / 0.92× → **1.00×** / 0.93× →
**1.03×**. bge-small's F32 row 9.40 → **10.19** at L512 b1, 0.85× → **0.92×**, 0.86× → **0.94×**,
0.90× → **0.95×**. The pass is 53.4 → **49.5 ms** (MiniLM) and 104.4 → **98.8 ms** (bge-small);
element-wise 9.31 → 7.54 and 17.78 → 15.98, attention 12.74 → 10.77 and 25.48 → 21.40. The other
four embedding families and both rerankers are inside their run-to-run band (rerank +1–2 %).

**What is left.** MiniLM is level to ahead on every 512-token cell (0.99–1.01×) and behind only
at L128 b8 (0.97×). bge-small is still 0.91–0.95× at 512 and 0.93–0.95× at 128 b1/b8, and the
reason is now one step: its exact-`erf` GELU is 10.7 of a 98.8 ms pass (10.8 %) against a fused
approximation, and its 12 layers pay 25 LayerNorms against the MVM nodes' 0.81 ms. Closing that
means changing the activation's arithmetic, which the engine's bit rule does not allow.


## 22. S4.9s as built: the long-text value mix's row pair (2026-11-12)

The cache-off ARM table's single cell behind 1.0 is Qwen3-Embedding 0.6B **F16** at one
2 000-token text, 0.96× llama.cpp (512 tokens 1.06×, 128 tokens 1.14×). This is its account,
the one same-bits change the account found, and the re-measured row. Raw runs, the stage
profiles, the harness and the per-round A/B: `benchmarks/results/spark-arm-2026-11-12-s4.9s/`.

**The account** (one thread on a Cortex-X925, `cellprof` + `PERGEGRINE_PROF2=full`, three runs,
medians; 528 and 2 000 tokens). The model is 28 blocks of d 1 024 / ff 3 072, 16 query heads and
8 KV heads of width 128, and its embedding is the causal prefill, so attention is causal:

| cell | wall | matmul | attention | mix | scores | other |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 528 tokens | 7.37 s | 6.25 s (84.8 %) | 0.95 s (12.8 %) | 0.64 s | 0.27 s | 0.17 s |
| 2 000 tokens | 37.44 s | 23.63 s (63.1 %) | 13.14 s (35.1 %) | **8.94 s (23.9 %)** | 3.95 s | 0.64 s |

In GMAC/s against this core's measured roof (`fpbench`: packed F16 47.0–47.6 GMAC/s, f32
62.6–65.8, and so a f32 FMA roof near 66): the **F16 matmul is 37.3**, i.e. 80 % of its own
kernel — the kernel is the matmul's limit, and it is 25–30 % under the f32 one because it widens
each panel first (S4.9p's trade). The **value mix is 12.8 GMAC/s, 19 % of the f32 roof**, the
score kernel 29.0 on the same tile. The mix is the losing stage, and the reason is its loop
shape, not the arithmetic: `mix_f16` walks positions outermost and dimensions innermost, so for
every key it loads the row's output, multiplies the stored V half by the softmax weight, adds and
stores the output back, and a second row sharing the KV head loads the same V half again. One
row therefore reads V once per row — **1.02 GB per head at 2 000 positions, 459 GB for the
mix** — where llama.cpp's `ggml_compute_forward_flash_attn_ext_tiled` (v0.9.0,
`ggml/src/ggml-cpu/ops.cpp:9080`) walks a **64 query-row × 64-key tile**, widens each K/V tile
to f32 once and reuses it across all 64 rows. Sharing across 2 rows instead of 64 is the
structural gap, and it is why the deficit appears at long text and not at 128 tokens.

**The change.** `crates/peregrine-kernels/src/neon.rs`'s `mix_f16_pair` (with
`mix_f16_pair_scalar` as the definition) turns the loop inside out: one eight-dimension slice of
both rows' outputs stays in registers while the keys walk ascending, and each stored V
half-vector is widened once for both rows. Each row keeps its own accumulator, its own α
multiply and its own separate multiply-then-add per key in ascending order (no FMA is
introduced), so no bit moves; a new test holds the pair to `mix_f16` **and** the scalar twin for
every length 1..=70, 127/128/129 and 511/512/513, at widths 64/128/72/80/16 and the edge alphas a
softmax produces, `to_bits` equal. `attn.rs`'s `run_rows` calls it when two rows of one KV head
see the same positions on the F16-direct tile (every aarch64 prefill). No golden changed.

**Measured** (two builds, five interleaved rounds, one thread; every round within ±1 % of its
arm's median): the mix 8.93 → **5.39 s** (−39.7 %) and its rate 12.8 → **21.3 GMAC/s**, attention
13.00 → **9.43 s** (−27.5 %), scores flat (3.94 → 3.93), matmul flat (23.09 → 23.00), the whole

arm 3.8×, so the absolute A/B does not read; the same-run control, attention ÷ that run's
untouched matmul, is **0.58 main against 0.48 branch**, the direction and size the one-thread
numbers predict.

**The row** (ten threads, cache off, three rounds, this window; the quiet-host cache-off table
in brackets for shape): 16 tokens 2.87× [1.57], 128 0.88× [1.14], 512 0.94× [1.06], 2 000 0.82×
[0.96], batch 32×128 1.08× [1.17], mixed 1.03× [1.10]. Every cell is 1.7–2.8× below the quiet

## 23. S4.9t as built: the long-context attention tile's loop shapes (2026-11-12)

S4.9s closed the value mix's row pair and left the account's other half open: at 2 000 tokens a
Qwen3-Embedding F16 pass was 35 % attention, the score kernel ran at 29.0 GMAC/s and the mix
bucket at 21.3 — a third of this core's f32 roof each — and the structural gap to llama.cpp's
64 × 64 tile was named but not built. This round is the account stage by stage, the same-bits
bound of each stage that is limited by its *loop shape*, and the three changes that close the
score, mix and soft-max parts. Raw runs, the kernel bench and the A/Bs:
`benchmarks/results/spark-arm-2026-11-12-s4.9t/`.

**The account** (one thread, `cellprof` + `PERGEGRINE_PROF2=full`, medians of three; 528 and
2 000 tokens). The engine's "mix" bucket is the value mix plus the soft-max's maximum and
exponentials, which run between its pieces; the kernel bench puts the soft-max at 0.878 ns per
score element, which is 0.055 s and 0.787 s:

| stage | 528 tok | 2 000 tok | GMAC/s at 2 000 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| matmul (F16) | 6.01 s | 22.79 s | 37.4 (80 % of its own kernel) |
| scores | 0.272 s | 3.897 s | **29.4** |
| mix (kernel alone) | 0.331 s | 4.566 s | **25.1** |
| soft-max (max + exp + denominator) | 0.055 s | 0.787 s | 0.878 ns/element |
| the caller (tile decode, banking, merge) | 0.023 s | 0.106 s | 0.3 % of the pass |

Three of the four attention stages are at a third to a half of the roof, and each is limited by
its loop shape:

- **scores** keep two accumulators per row — dimensions `i..i+4` and `i+4..i+8` of each ascending
  eight-wide block, because the canonical tree accumulates lane-wise in ascending order and then
  reduces `((a0+a1)+(a2+a3)) + ((b0+b1)+(b2+b3))`. With a four-cycle FMA latency, two chains
  cover half the four FMA pipes; the row pair (two rows × two keys × two halves) had eight, which
  is why *it* looked healthy at 31.5.
- **the mix** is `o += p_j · v_j` with a rounded product and a rounded add — no FMA may be
  introduced — so it costs two operations per four products and its ceiling is half the pipes;
  and with two accumulators per row over an eight-dimension slice a row pair has four chains,
  which is a third of the machine. It measured 25.1.
- **the soft-max** is one ascending `f64` sum per row, and a scalar `f64` add waits four cycles:
  a row alone spends three of every four cycles idle. S4.9r had already measured that on the
  encoder path and given four rows four interleaved denominators; the decoder still ran one row
  at a time.

**The bound, and what the defined order permits.** Nothing in the ascending key order, the
canonical tree or the mix's separate multiply-then-add can move, so the only free variable is how
many independent chains are in flight, and how many rows one pass over the tile can share.
Chains = rows × keys × 2 halves for the scores and rows × 2 for the mix; sixteen covers the four
FMA pipes. Measured with a kernel bench (hd 128, 64-key pieces, GMAC/s): scores one row 24.2,
pair 31.5, four keys per row 33.0 (and eight keys per row spills, 23.7), **four rows × two keys
40.3**, four rows × four keys 25.8. Mix: pair 28.4, pair over a 16-dimension slice (eight chains)
33.7, pair over 32 dimensions 34.0, **four rows 54.8**, eight rows 53.2, sixteen 48.1 — the knee is
eight chains. But four rows can share a *mix* piece only if they share their context and their
chunk count: cutting a row anywhere but its own chunk boundary gives its soft-max an extra step,
which changes the denominator's `f64` summation. Qwen3-Embedding has two query heads per KV head
and one position per row, so the four-row group never forms there; a model with four query heads
per KV head (Llama 3.2 1B) forms it at every position.

**The changes.** (1) `neon.rs`'s `scores_f16_tile4` (with `scores_f16_tile4_scalar` as the
definition): four query rows over one F16 K tile, two keys per pass, sixteen accumulation chains,
the K tile read once for four rows; `attn.rs` groups four rows that see exactly the same part of
the tile, falling back to the pair and then the single row, and x86 keeps its own pair. (2)
`mix_f16_quad` (with `mix_f16_quad_scalar`): four rows over one F16 V tile, each stored V half
widened once for all four, each row keeping its own alpha, accumulator and ascending
multiply-then-add; the caller groups four rows of one KV head that share their context and chunk
count. (3) `softmax_rows` calls `bidir::exp_rows4_sums` — S4.9r's encoder body — when four rows
share a piece, interleaving four denominators on the same single bit-defined fold. Each commit
carries its own test: the score tile and the mix against their single-row bodies **and** their
twins over every length 1..=70, 127/128/129 and 511/512/513 at a packed and a padded stride, head
dims 64/128/80/72/16, plus the values a saturated soft-max leaves and the edge alphas; the
soft-max against row-at-a-time over the same lengths and every piece length up to `BLOCK`, with
the prior `m`/`l` a previous tile leaves, comparing the tile, the maximum, the denominator and
the rescale factor bit for bit. No golden file changed.

**Measured**, one thread. The score tile, five interleaved rounds of two builds: scores 3.914 →
**3.122 s** at 2 000 tokens (−20.2 %) and 0.274 → 0.223 s at 528 (−18.4 %), attention 9.372 →
8.606 s (−8.2 %), the 2 000-token pass 33.23 → 32.02 s (−3.6 %, the round-total table below reads
−2.1 % in a second window), with the mix bucket and matmul flat. The four-row mix and soft-max,
which this model cannot form, on Llama 3.2 1B Q4_K_M's prompt attention at 4 096 rows (five
interleaved rounds each): **+4.3 %** from the mix and **+9.4 %** from the soft-max. The pair's
16-dimension slice was built and **dropped**: 28.4 → 33.7 GMAC/s alone, +0.8 % in the pass.

**The rows**, two interleaved rounds of the two builds (one-thread; every cell's 1-minute load was
between 4 and 8, so each is marked loaded): Qwen3-Embedding F16 528 tokens 6.862 → 6.828 s
(−0.5 %), 2 000 tokens 32.80 → **32.10 s** (−2.1 %), its attention −8.1 %; the Q8_0 file of the
same model 3.285 → 3.253 s and 19.35 → **18.69 s** (−3.4 %); gte-modernbert-base F16 (the encoder
guard) +0.5 % and +0.3 %, i.e. inside the window's spread and untouched by construction. The
decoder guard — Llama 3.2 1B Q4_K_M, a 4 096-token prompt, `promptbench` — goes 63.97 → **69.02
tok/s at one thread (+7.9 %)** with its attention 24.80 → 20.30 s; at ten threads that window's
untouched matmul moves 13 % between arms, so its cell is host state and is marked so.

**What is left.** The ten-thread engine table against llama.cpp — the Qwen3-Embedding F16 and Q8_0
rows at 16/128/512/2 000 tokens, the 32 × 128 batch, the mixed batch and the gte guard — is
**owed**: no window of this round had the load under 4, so `docs/benchmark.md`'s ARM row is
unchanged and none of this round's cells is a standing. And the structural gap S4.9s named is now
the only one left in attention: llama.cpp reads a 64-row tile's K and V once, Peregrine reads a V
tile once per row-group, and widening that group past the query heads a position owns would give
the online soft-max extra steps — it changes bits, so it is a different round's problem, not a
loop shape.
