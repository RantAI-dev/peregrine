# S5.1: the AVX-512 Q4_K and Q5_0 kernels, as built

- Status: built and measured, no result moved
- Date: 2026-11-11
- Host: AMD Ryzen AI 9 HX 370 (Zen 5, 12 cores / 24 threads, AVX-512 F/BW/VL/VNNI/VBMI), WSL2
- Baseline: `origin/main` `ab54f4e`
- Raw measurements: `benchmarks/results/desktop-zen5-2026-11-11-s5.1-epilogue/`

This note is the "as built" half of item 1 of `docs/design/s5-prefill-avx512.md` §6 (PR #194).
That study is not on `main` yet, so the note lives beside it rather than as a section of it; when

## 1. What the study asked for

The Q4_K AVX-512 kernel ran at 76–79 GMAC/s whatever the number of activation rows, against
ik_llama.cpp's 134–148, and its rate was flat from 16 to 128 rows — so "batching amortises
nothing". §6 item 1 said to move the min term, the scale decode and the float fold out of the
per-activation-row epilogue, at a target of 76 → about 120 GMAC/s per core, bit-identical.

## 2. What was built

**The min term is two sub-blocks per `vpdpwssd`.** `KqActs` gains `bsp`, the per-32-weight
activation sums packed two to a lane (`S[2p]` low, `S[2p+1]` high, both int16), filled once with
the rest of the interleaved activation. The tile's six-bit mins are paired the same way, once per
super-block (`q4k8_minpairs`). A row's min term is then four `vpdpwssd` against four broadcasts
instead of eight `vpmulld` and eight `vpaddd` against eight scalar broadcasts. Every product is at
most `63 · 4064` and every partial sum at most `2 048 256`, so the int32 chain is exact and gives
the same integer the eight multiplies gave — and the fold after it is byte for byte the same
expression. The one-row path builds its four pairs from `QuantAct::bsums` with two `vpmaddwd`
instead, so it does not pay for the wider layout.

**The prefill group is eight activation rows, and the window loop is outside the row loop.** A
super-block's weight windows, scale decode and min pairs are now shared by eight rows instead of
four. The loop order is what makes that pay: for each of the four window groups, the four decoded
weight vectors are loaded and then every activation row runs its own `vpdpbusd` chain against
them, so sixteen independent chains are in flight. (A first attempt at eight rows walked the rows
outermost and was 8 % *slower*: two chains in flight per row, and the kernel is latency-bound on
`vpdpbusd`, whose measured latency here is about seven cycles.) Twelve rows measured no faster and
sixteen slower — the sixteen int32 accumulators plus the scales stop fitting.

**Q5_0 gets the same wider group.** `q5_08_gemm_zmm` works eight rows per pass, sharing each
block's fifth-bit spread and activation broadcasts. It is the same function with `N = 8`; the
remainders keep `N = 7..1`.

**The activation's addressing is worked out once per row.** A new `ActSrc::codes_at` +
`codes_stride` + `window_step` lets the kernel divide the row by the interleaved group width once
per call instead of once per (row, window). It measured neutral on its own and is kept because the
eight-row group makes the per-window work relatively more important.

**Rejected after measuring, all bit-identical and all slower or flat:** the dpwssd min term
*alone* (four rows, no wider group: 74 → 78 GMAC/s, i.e. nothing); rows-outermost groups; twelve
and sixteen rows; a pre-broadcast activation buffer (a 64-byte load per `vpdpbusd` instead of a
`vpbroadcastq`, 65 against 78 GMAC/s); prefetching the next super-block (inside the noise).

## 3. The kernel, before and after

`kqbench`, `taskset -c 2`, one tile of sixteen super-blocks (18 KiB, L1), median of five
interleaved rounds of old and new. GMAC/s per core; 2 048 MACs per (tile, activation row,
super-block).

| kernel | 1 act | 4 acts | 16 acts | 64 acts | 128 acts |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q4_K tile, ZMM, before | 43.5 | 76.7 | 76.8 | 74.6 | 73.6 |
| **Q4_K tile, ZMM, after** | **44.0** | **78.6** | **80.8** | **79.3** | **78.2** |
| Q4_K one-row GEMV, before → after | 43.5 → 44.0 | — | — | — | — |
| Q5_0 tile, ZMM, before | 24.6 | 49.2 | 48.4 | 47.8 | 46.3 |
| **Q5_0 tile, ZMM, after** | **24.7** | **49.2** | **54.2** | **54.3** | **52.1** |
| Q4_K tile, AVX2 (control) | 30.4 | 51.7 | 56.6 | 54.7 | 54.9 |
| ik_llama.cpp Q4_K_R4 (`§1.6`, for reference) | 73.2 | — | 134.0 | 113.6 | 148.1 |

So: Q4_K +5 % at the sixteen-row chunk, +6 % at 64–128 rows; Q5_0 +12–14 % from sixteen rows up.
The target of 120 GMAC/s was **not** reached. The one-row decode kernel did not regress.

The same probe on a tile of 32 768 super-blocks (36 MiB, DRAM) — the working set the engine has,
because chunk 16 streams every weight matrix once per pass — collapses most of the gain:

| kernel | 16 acts | 64 acts | 128 acts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q4_K, ZMM, before | 71.3 | 69.4 | 69.5 |
| Q4_K, ZMM, after | 71.9 | 71.5 | 73.3 |
| Q5_0, ZMM, before | 42.8 | 41.9 | 42.0 |
| Q5_0, ZMM, after | 42.0 | 42.8 | 41.4 |

At that size the kernel is bound by the weight stream, not by its own arithmetic, so a faster
kernel buys between nothing and +5 %.

## 4. End to end

`peregrine bench --prompt-tokens 512 --tokens 4 --no-profile`, medians of interleaved rounds of
the two binaries (7 rounds at twelve threads, 3 at one), prompt tok/s.

| model | threads | before | after | ratio | ik_llama.cpp (study §1.3) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen 2.5 0.5B Q4_K_M | 12 | 457.8 | 485.4 | **1.060** | 1158.3 |
| Llama 3.2 1B Q4_K_M | 12 | 282.9 | 288.8 | **1.021** | 549.9 |
| Qwen 2.5 0.5B Q4_K_M | 1 | 94.7 | 100.1 | **1.058** | 183.9 |
| Llama 3.2 1B Q4_K_M | 1 | 48.4 | 50.7 | **1.047** | 82.0 |

Decode, same harness: 1.002 (Qwen) and 0.998 (1B) at twelve threads in that series, and 1.007 /
0.997 in a decode-only series of seven interleaved rounds with 64 tokens — no regression.

Peregrine ÷ ik_llama.cpp for the 512-token prompt, after: **2.39 → 2.39** for Qwen (1158.3 /
485.4) and **1.94 → 1.90** for the 1B (549.9 / 288.8). The ratio barely moves, because the
kernel was not the engine's limit at this chunk: the Q5_0 gain on Qwen is the one that shows.

## 5. Why the gap did not close, and what is left

Measured, not inferred:

- **The kernel is not the engine's cost at chunk 16.** A 16-token pass reads every weight matrix
  once, so the matmul streams the whole model per pass and the kernel runs at its DRAM-resident
  rate (§3's second table), where its arithmetic is a minority of the time.
- **The epilogue was not the cost either.** Removing the min term entirely from the four-row
  kernel bought 10 %, removing the scale multiply 18 %, and a quarter fewer instructions in the
  min term bought nothing at all — the `vpmulld` and the `vpdpbusd` ports are what the kernel
  waits on, and the fix was more chains in flight, not fewer instructions.
- **What is left, in order of expected value:** (1) a wider pass (S5.1's own PR 3): at 64–128
  activation rows the kernel is 6–14 % faster *and* the weight stream is amortised 4–8×, which is
  where the 2× lives; (2) two eight-row tiles per pass, which halves the activation broadcasts
  and so the kernel's instruction count per MAC — it needs the caller to hand the kernel sixteen
  output rows and a 16×`MAX_BATCH` scratch; (3) a sixteen-output-row tile, which puts one int32
  lane per row and quarters the scale multiplies per output.

## 6. Bits

No golden moved. The integer sums are order-free, the dpwssd's products are the same integers,
and the fold is the same expression with the same operands in the same order, so every row is the
bit its AVX2 twin produces at every batch width. `the_avx512_kernel_agrees_with_the_avx2_one_bit_for_bit`
and its Q5_0 twin now walk one to forty activation rows (five whole eight-row groups, every
remainder), and their `has_no_cross_row_state` twins start a batch inside a four-row group, inside
an eight-row group, and on either side of the wide group's boundary. The golden test passes
unchanged on this machine for `x86_64-avx2-avx512-vnni-gfni-vbmi`, `x86_64-avx2` and `x86_64`
(scalar) on the three model files this machine holds whose bytes match their goldens. The fourth,
SmolLM2-360M, already differed before this change: the file here is 270 590 880 bytes and the
golden was recorded from a 270 590 976-byte one.
