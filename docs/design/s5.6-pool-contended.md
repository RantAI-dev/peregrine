# The pool on a contended host (S5.6)

A fork–join pool over borrowed data must wait for every worker it has called. On a
quiet machine that is free: the workers are all running. On a shared one it is the
product: a worker the host has descheduled holds the join open for a scheduling
timeslice, and a decode step is ~160 joins, so one busy co-tenant thread turned
124 tok/s into 9.9.

This note is the account of that loss on the ARM host (ten Cortex-X925 cores shared
with other tenants), the two mechanisms that remove it, what each is worth disturbed
and undisturbed, and what was measured and dropped. The raw numbers and the driver
are in `benchmarks/results/spark-arm-2026-11-13-s5.6/`.

## 1. The problem, measured with a controlled disturbance

`peregrine-bench`'s `loadgen` example runs `N` busy threads (0, 1, 2, 4) pinned with
`taskset` to the same ten CPUs the engine is pinned to (5–9, 15–19), for exactly one
measurement; the driver starts it before a cell and stops it by PID in a `finally`.
Every record carries the 1-minute load average and the per-core idle share of those
ten CPUs over the run's own window. Rounds interleave the levels, so a co-tenant that
arrives or leaves affects each level alike.

Cells: Llama 3.2 1B Q4_K_M prompt tok/s at 512 and 4 096 tokens and decode tok/s,
and gte-modernbert-base F16 on 32 different 128-token texts with the exact result
cache off, at 4, 8 and 10 threads, pinned and (10 threads) unpinned. llama.cpp
v0.9.0 and ik_llama.cpp `479074c` are the yardsticks. medians of three to five runs;
full table and spreads in the results README.

**Peregrine before, own fraction of its quiet cell (pinned, ten threads):**

| cell | 0 | 1 | 2 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| prefill 512 | 1.000 | 0.297 | 0.281 | 0.390 |
| prefill 4 096 | 1.000 | 0.349 | 0.326 | 0.452 |
| decode | 1.000 | **0.079** | 0.476 | 0.515 |
| embed 32×128 | 1.000 | **0.119** | 0.198 | 0.308 |

llama.cpp loses less in the same windows (0.89/0.80/0.61 on prefill 512,
0.88/0.80/0.60 on 4 096, 0.63/0.58/0.42 decode, 0.71/0.63/0.49 embed), and
ik_llama.cpp loses more on its much faster kernels (0.42/0.35/0.28 prefill 512 at
ten threads against a quiet 1 097 tok/s). One busy thread, not ten, is the worst
case for Peregrine: with eleven runnable threads on ten cores exactly one worker is
off-CPU at every join, and every join pays a wake-up.

## 2. The account

The pool's own counters (`PEREGRINE_PROF2=1`, `PEREGRINE_ADAPTIVE_LOG=1`) on a
4 096-token prefill at ten threads, 12 385 joins:

| | quiet | 1 disturber | 4 disturbers |
|---|---|---|---|
| wall | 7.82 s | 22.65 s | 17.41 s |
| caller's post-share wait | 1.35 s (17 %) | **15.20 s (67 %)** | 7.57 s (43 %) |
| wait per join | 108 µs | **1 227 µs** | 611 µs |
| straggler gap (last worker − second), mean / max | — | **0.97 / 3.82 ms** | — |
| which worker was last | — | uniform over 1–9 | uniform over 1–9 |

The late worker is not one core or one pinned worker: the histogram over 12 385
joins is flat across all nine workers (1 047–1 712 each), so the host deschedules
whichever worker it likes. The gap between the last worker to finish and the one
before it is ~1 ms — a scheduler timeslice, not the tens of microseconds of ordinary
imbalance — at almost every join.

**The work split is not the cause, and neither is the schedstat ladder.** The linear
matmuls already claim dynamic chunks (51 rows at 4 096/10), so a late worker holds at
most one chunk; attention and the norms use static shares, but their shares are
small next to the 1 ms wait. What holds the join open is the *barrier contract*: a
worker that has not arrived still has to run and decrement `remaining` before the
caller may return, and a worker the host has taken away cannot do that until it is
scheduled. The spin window then makes it worse: nine finished workers spin on the
epoch while the late worker needs the core.

The adaptive-spin ladder did **not** react at one disturber. Its gate is the *waiter's*
own run-queue delay, sampled from `/proc/thread-self/schedstat`; at one busy thread
that read 85–207 permille, under the 350 permille gate, so the pool stayed at the
1 ms window while the join waited 67 % of its wall. Forcing `PEREGRINE_SPIN_US=0`
recovered 181 → 305 tok/s on the spot, which names the spin as the first cause and
the barrier as the second: 305 is still 41 % below quiet.

## 3. Mechanism 1: park when the join itself stalls

The signal is the one the pool already has for free: the caller's wait after its own
share, as a fraction of the whole fork–join. A quiet ten-thread 4 096-token pass
waits 13–17 % (ordinary imbalance); a descheduled worker pushes it past 60 %. The
gate also requires the host's smoothed run-queue delay to be at least 80 permille, so
a workload whose rows are simply uneven (a batch of texts of different lengths, a
pooled pass waiting for another slot) cannot be mistaken for a descheduled worker.

Four consecutive stalled joins (`wait ≥ 50 µs` and `wait ≥ 40 %` of the join, with
the host gate open) set a `forced_park` flag: every waiter — workers between jobs and
the caller — takes the parking rung, whatever the spin ladder says. After a hold of
unstalled joins (512, doubling to 8 192 after a failed probe) the window is tested
again; a probe that stalls parks immediately and doubles the hold. This is
`PEREGRINE_ADAPTIVE_STALL=0` to turn off (it also disables mechanism 2).

Measured on the 4 096-token prefill, ten threads, one run per cell, same host window:

| | before | park on join stall | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| quiet | 524.5 | 521.0 | −0.7 % (within spread) |
| 1 disturber | 181.7 | 311.1 | **+71 %** |
| 4 disturbers | 235.5 | 241.5 | +2.5 % |

Nothing in it changes what a worker computes: only how long a waiter spins before it
parks. The quiet path adds a clock pair per join and one atomic load.

## 4. Mechanism 2: claimed partitions, and a join that does not wait for an absent worker

Parking removes the self-inflicted part; the barrier is still there. The second
mechanism changes the contract for jobs that can be split into independent units:

* The pool publishes a *claimed* job: `run_claimed(units, claim, f)`.
* The caller and every worker that arrives take `[lo, hi)` ranges from one counter;
  `f` writes only its own range, exactly as the static and dynamic partitions did,
  so the bits do not depend on who took which range.
* The caller returns when the counter is exhausted and no range is in flight. A
  worker the host has not scheduled yet is not waited for: before returning, the
  caller publishes the job's generation in `closed`, and a late worker that observes
  it skips the closure entirely. A worker that *has* entered is counted in `busy`
  and is waited for, so the closure cannot be freed under it.
* The job's fields (data pointer, trampoline, units, claim, generation) live in
  atomics bracketed by a sequence counter, and a worker retries its read if a
  publish crossed it. This matters only because claimed jobs are the first jobs whose
  caller may return before every worker has observed them: without the sequence
  lock, a worker descheduled across a job boundary could mix one generation's call
  pointer with another's data. (The first version did exactly that: it hung and then
  corrupted memory in the parallel synthetic-model tests. The sequence lock and the
  never-reset `busy` counter are the fix.)

Blocks use the claimed form only while mechanism 1 says the pool is stalled
(`Pool::claiming()`): `for_row_chunks`, `for_row_chunks_2`, `for_row_chunks_3`, the
linear matmul's dynamic path, the packed-float unit path, the attention task loops
(including flash-decode), and the fused SwiGLU of a decode step. On a healthy host
every block keeps the partition it was tuned with, so the quiet path is byte-for-byte
the same scheduling code as before.

Measured on the same grid:

| | before | park only | park + claim | quiet cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| prefill 4 096, 1 disturber | 181.7 | 311.1 | **443.9** | 521.0 vs 524.5 |
| prefill 4 096, 4 disturbers | 235.5 | 241.5 | **323.8** | |
| prefill 512, 1 disturber | 213.6 | — | **606.4** | 725.2 vs 719.0 |
| decode, 1 disturber | 9.9 | — | **102.6** | 124.0 vs 124.5 |
| decode, 4 disturbers | 64.1 | — | **89.0** | |
| embed 32×128, 1 disturber | 2.66 | — | **17.4** | 22.6 vs 22.3 |
| embed 32×128, 4 disturbers | 6.87 | — | **13.3** | |

The caller's wait on the 4 096-token pass falls from 15.2 s (67 %) to 0.78 s (8.7 %)
at one disturber and from 7.6 s (43 %) to 0.91 s (7.4 %) at four. What remains is not
a wait: every stage runs 15–30 % slower because eleven or fourteen runnable threads
share ten cores (and the X925 cluster's clock drops under all-core load). The
straggler gap's mean falls from 0.97 ms to 19 µs.

The quiet check is the one that mattered: the first version of the stall signal used
the wait ratio alone, and the embedding cell (whose pooled passes are legitimately
imbalanced) tripped it. With the run-queue gate, a controlled A/B of the same binary
with `PEREGRINE_ADAPTIVE_STALL=0` against `=1` on the quiet embedding cell gives
22.57 against 22.21 texts/s (within spread); the quiet prefill and decode cells are
unchanged.

### Bits

A row's bits never depend on the threads or the split: the claimed ranges are
disjoint by construction, exactly like the static and dynamic partitions they
replace, and every kernel computes a row independently of its neighbours. The
existing invariance and golden tests were run under the change (native and the
`neon-dotprod`, `neon`, `scalar` caps). As a direct check under the mechanism,
`peregrine run` with the same 4 000-byte prompt and 64 greedy tokens at ten threads
produced byte-identical text with the pool quiet and with four disturbers (the
claimed path active). No `.golden` file changed.

### Added in review

- **The sequence lock's ordering.** The job fields are relaxed atomics between two increments
  of the sequence, read with a relaxed load each and validated by a second read of the
  sequence. On a weakly ordered CPU (aarch64, the machine this was built on) that leaves two
  reorderings open: a field store may become visible before the odd sequence, and a field load
  may be satisfied after the validating load. `publish` now issues a release fence after the
  odd increment and `read_job` an acquire fence before the validating load, the discipline
  crossbeam's `SeqLock` uses. A torn pair here is a call pointer of one closure type with
  another closure's data, so this is soundness, not tuning.
- **The claimed partitions are now under the goldens.** A quiet host never takes them, so no
  test did. `PEREGRINE_ADAPTIVE_STALL=claim` makes `Pool::claiming` true on every fork–join,
  and CI runs the synthetic and embedding golden suites a second time with it: every golden
  must come out of the claimed partitions bit for bit.
- **The encoder strip's scratch slot.** `blocks::bidir` fetched its per-worker slot with
  `Disjoint::chunk`, which the debug overlap tracker counts as a writer per fetch; a claimed
  job enters the closure once per range, so a debug build under claiming reported an overlap
  that is not one. It takes the slot with `window_mut` now, as attention does with
  `unclaimed_mut`.
- **With S5.4j's join-gap window.** S5.4j (merged first) stretches the spin window to the
  measured gap between joins, up to 8 ms. A forced park still wins (`budgets` returns zero
  before it looks at the gap), and a claimed job marks its own start and end for the gap
  measure. The disturbed cells of section 4 were measured without the stretched window: the
  re-read on the merged build is owed.

## 5. What was tried and dropped

* **An effective width that follows the host.** The candidate is to drop one worker
  at a time when joins stall. The thread sweep is its ceiling: at one disturber ten
  threads beats nine and eight (449.6 / 421.0 / 369.7 tok/s), and at four disturbers
  eight beats ten (346.2 / 321.6 in a single run, 327.3 / 323.8 in the grid, i.e.
  within spread). A width ladder would need its own controller and could not win at
  both levels; the claimed join already removes the wait that a narrower pool would
  reduce. Dropped.
* **A finer dynamic grain for attention.** Converting attention's static task shares
  to claimed units is part of mechanism 2; a *finer* grain (claim 1 per (head, row))
  was not measured separately because the attention task is already one head's rows
  and the 4 096-token pass's remaining loss is clock, not wait.
* **The schedstat ladder as the trigger.** It did not react at one disturber (85–207
  permille against a 350 gate). It is kept as a *gate* for the new signal, not as the
  signal.
* **Waiting less by yielding (`PEREGRINE_YIELD_US`).** A yielding waiter still
  competes with the straggler; the measured win was parking, so nothing was built on
  yield.
* **More threads under four disturbers.** No: the grid's t10 is within spread of t8
  on prefill (323.8 against 327.3) and better on decode (89.0 against 83.6); asking
  for more threads is not slower than asking for fewer beyond the spread.

## 6. What remains

* **The clock, not the scheduler.** At four disturbers a 4 096-token pass is 63 % of
  quiet and llama.cpp is 60 %; the gap is the shared cluster's frequency and cache,
  not a wait (the caller's wait is 7 %). A product answer is fewer threads only when
  the host is oversubscribed, which the width candidate did not pay for.
* **One disturber is still the worst case** (0.83–0.86 of quiet on prefill against
  llama.cpp's 0.88–0.89). The residual is 11 runnable threads on ten cores plus the
  cluster clock; the wait is gone.
* **Non-claimed blocks.** The recurrent (hybrid models), MoE, dspark and LittleBit
  paths still use the whole-job barrier. They are not in these cells; the same two
  mechanisms apply if a measured cell needs them.
* **Other platforms.** The stall signal does not use `/proc` (the run-queue gate
  does, and is Linux-only), so on macOS and Windows the mechanism is off until a
  host-load signal exists there; the claimed partitions themselves are portable.

## 7. The re-read on the merged build

S5.4j landed after section 4's table was measured, and its join-gap spin window
(`GAP_CAP_US = 8 000` µs, §5l of the fast-prompt note) changes the one thing section 4
chose: how long a worker spins before it parks. The re-read is
`benchmarks/results/spark-arm-2026-11-13-s5.6-reread/`: the same `loadgen`
disturbance, cells and ten-thread pinning, with three builds interleaved **inside
every round** — `head` (`7d46020`, S5.6 before the merge), `merged`
(`origin/s5.6-pool-contended`) and `gap0` (the merged branch with `GAP_CAP_US = 0`,
a local build) — five rounds, medians and spreads from `summarize.py`.

**The stretch costs prefill on this host.** `merged` is 1.2–2.6 % below `gap0` in all
eight prefill cells, quiet and disturbed, and the deficit is larger than `gap0`'s
half-range at every one (at 4 096: 0.979–0.988 against spans of 0.1–0.5 %). Decode
and the embedding cell are level (+0.0–0.5 %). **The merge does not keep S5.6's
prefill numbers:** `merged` is 1.0–2.5 % below `head` on prefill, while `gap0` —
the same build with the stretch off — is level with `head` (0.996–1.021). The quiet
cells are therefore **not** level either: 717.8 / 731.6 / 729.3 tok/s at 512 tokens
and 512.8 / 521.3 / 518.6 at 4 096 (`merged` / `gap0` / `head`). On this host the
stretch costs with the run queue empty, which is the opposite sign from S5.4j's
desktop measurement (+3.3 %) and is why the re-read was owed: the desktop's joins are
1–5 ms apart on a quiet machine, this host's are the same but its ten cores are never
quiet enough for the extra spin to be free.

**Fixed: the stretch stops when the host is oversubscribed.** `budgets` now takes
`configured.max(gap_us())` only while the smoothed run-queue delay is below
[`STALL_GATE_PERMILLE`] (80) — the gate section 3 already trusts. A forced park still
returns zero first, and a configured window of zero is still left alone. The reason is
section 3's: when more threads are runnable than there are cores, a longer spin takes
a core from the worker the join is waiting for. The instrumented runs put the reading
at 0 permille on the quiet 4 096 cell, 70–133 at one disturber (across the gate) and
366–476 at four.

Same grid, same window, with the fix (`fixed`, `merged`, `gap0`; medians of five):

| cell | level | merged | fixed | gap0 | fixed/gap0 | fixed/merged |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| prefill 512 | 0 | 715.5 | 718.5 | 728.2 | 0.987 | 1.004 |
| prefill 512 | 1 | 596.2 | 601.7 | 606.8 | 0.992 | 1.009 |
| prefill 512 | 2 | 540.0 | 554.9 | 558.0 | 0.995 | 1.027 |
| prefill 512 | 4 | 434.4 | 438.8 | 442.9 | 0.991 | 1.010 |
| prefill 4 096 | 0 | 509.9 | 512.9 | 516.8 | 0.992 | 1.006 |
| prefill 4 096 | 1 | 438.4 | 441.6 | 446.9 | 0.988 | 1.007 |
| prefill 4 096 | 2 | 409.9 | 412.6 | 417.1 | 0.989 | 1.006 |
| prefill 4 096 | 4 | 320.8 | 321.9 | 324.9 | 0.991 | 1.004 |

The fix recovers 0.4–2.7 % of the stretch's cost under disturbance (largest at 512
tokens and two disturbers: 540.0 → 554.9) and leaves `fixed` within 0.4–1.2 % of
`gap0` in every prefill cell. **What is left is the quiet-cell stretch**: the
run-queue reading there is 0 permille, so the gate never closes and the ~1 % that
`merged` loses to `gap0` quiet stays. This host has no signal that separates its
"quiet" from the desktop's, so the next lever is a cap tied to the join wait itself
rather than the run queue — written down, not taken. The embedding cell's
four-disturber level is the one place the two windows disagree (the after window ran
at load up to 13.9 against the before window's 11.4), so its ratio there is not read
as an arm difference.

Nothing in the fix changes what a worker computes: it shortens a spin, and the
`pool::tests` case `the_gap_stretch_stops_on_an_oversubscribed_host` pins that the
stretch returns when the co-tenant leaves. No golden moved.

**Decided in review.** The gate above covers the oversubscribed half. For the quiet half
the evidence is per machine and has opposite signs: +3.3 % on the AVX-512 desktop's fast
512-token cell (S5.4j), level on the four-core AVX2 host (0.98–1.04, S4.9w's Part 0), and
1.6–2.0 % slower on this host's quiet ten-thread prefill. The stretch is therefore **off on
aarch64** (`GAP_STRETCH` in `pool.rs`; the gap is not even timed there) and on for x86-64,
which puts this host's prefill cells back where S5.6 measured them. An aarch64 machine with
no co-tenants has not been measured; the switch is the place to revisit when one is.
